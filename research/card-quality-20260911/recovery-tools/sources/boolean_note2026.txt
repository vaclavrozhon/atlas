                                                Sharp Analysis of Gaussian Rounding for
                                                          Boolean Max 𝑘 -CSP
                                                                             Yury Makarychev (TTIC)


                                                 In this note, we show that the approximation algorithm for Boolean Max 𝑘-CSP presented
                                              in [7] yields a (1 − 𝑜 𝑘 (1))𝑘/2 𝑘 approximation, as conjectured in [8]. This improves the
                                              previous guarantee of (0.626612 − 𝑜 𝑘 (1))𝑘/2 𝑘 from [7] and asymptotically matches the
                                              known hardness results; see [8, 1, 5, 3]. The result is a short corollary of the Gaussian
                                              stochastic domination theorem of Mulgund [9].

                                        1 Introduction
                                        For Boolean Max 𝑘-CSP, Hast [6] gave an Ω(𝑘/(2 𝑘 log 𝑘)) approximation, and Charikar, Makarychev,
                                        and Makarychev [4] obtained a 𝑐𝑘/2 𝑘 approximation for an absolute constant 𝑐 > 0.44. In [7] we
                                        improved the constant to 0.626612−𝑜 𝑘 (1) and conjectured that the same approach gives a (1−𝑜 𝑘 (1))𝑘/2 𝑘
                                        approximation; see also the survey [8].
                                          The approximation guarantee is asymptotically optimal. Austrin and Mossel [1] showed, assuming the
                                        Unique Games Conjecture, hardness of approximation within (𝑘 + 𝑂 (𝑘 0.525 ))/2 𝑘 + 𝜀. De and Mossel [5]
                                        subsequently improved this to (𝑘 + 1)/2 𝑘 + 𝜀 for odd 𝑘 and (𝑘 + 2)/2 𝑘 + 𝜀 for even 𝑘. Thus, under
                                        the Unique Games Conjecture, the optimal approximation factor is (1 + 𝑜 𝑘 (1))𝑘/2 𝑘 . Chan [3] obtained
                                        unconditional hardness from pairwise-independent subgroups; in particular, for 𝑘 = 2𝑟 − 1, his result
                                        gives hardness within (𝑘 + 1)/2 𝑘 + 𝜀. Hence the constant in our approximation guarantee is optimal
                                        under 𝑃 ≠ 𝑁 𝑃 for an infinite sequence of values of 𝑘.
                                        Overview. We use the same SDP relaxation, notation, and Gaussian rounding as in [7]. The only new
                                        ingredient is Mulgund’s stochastic domination theorem [9], which we use as a black box. If 𝐶 is a clause
                                        and ∥𝑧𝐶 ∥ 2 = 𝛽2 /𝑘, the theorem gives the key estimate
