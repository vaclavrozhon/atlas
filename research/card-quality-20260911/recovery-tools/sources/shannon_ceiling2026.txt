                                         1        Introduction
                                         A perfect secret-sharing scheme distributes a secret among a set of participants so that every
                                         authorised coalition can reconstruct the secret, whereas every unauthorised coalition has no
                                         information about it. A central problem is to determine how large the shares must be for the
                                         most difficult access structures.
                                             Csirmaz constructed explicit n-participant access structures for which one share has size
                                         Ω(n/ log n) times the secret size [Csi97]. His related total-share construction gives a lower bound
                                         of Ω(n2 / log n) [Csi96]; this was still described as the best general total-share lower bound in
                                         2023 [Bei23]. We exhibit an explicit family attaining linear maximum-share complexity and
                                         quadratic total-share complexity. To the best of our knowledge, these improve the previously
                                         published general lower bounds by a logarithmic factor.                               √
                                             Applebaum and Nir observed that an explicit monotone formula of size L on O( L) variables
                                         with total secret-sharing complexity Ω(L) would remove a logarithmic loss from their hardness
                                         result [AN25, Remark 2.2]. Our family has precisely these parameters.
                                             The access structure has three cyclically indexed classes of participants, denoted Xi , Yi , hi .
                                         Each helper hs selects a perfect matching between the X- and Y -participants, and a coalition
                                         containing hs is authorised when it contains an endpoint of every edge in that matching.

2     Entropy identities and secret-sharing inequalities
Let P be a finite participant set. A secret-sharing scheme consists of a jointly distributed
secret S and shares (Za )a∈P , with H(S) > 0. For a coalition A ⊆ P, write ZA = (Za )a∈A . To
reduce notation, after this section we write H(A) for H(ZA ) and write a participant label a
in place of its share Za . Thus Aa denotes A ∪ {a}, and juxtaposition denotes union of named
coalitions. For random variables U1 , . . . , Um , juxtaposition denotes their joint distribution; thus
H(U W ) = H(U, W ), and more generally H(AU W ) denotes the joint entropy of all random
variables represented by A, U , and W .
    The scheme is perfect for an access structure Γ if

                      H(S | ZA ) = 0    (A ∈ Γ),        I(S; ZA ) = 0   (A ∈
                                                                           / Γ).

For an unauthorised coalition, the second condition is equivalently H(S | ZA ) = H(S).
   We use the following standard identities. For random variables U, V, W :

                      H(U | W ) = H(U W ) − H(W ),                                                  (1)
                    I(U ; V | W ) = H(U | W ) − H(U | V W )                                         (2)
                                   = H(U W ) + H(V W ) − H(W ) − H(U V W ).                         (3)

The last line follows from the preceding two by expanding

            H(U | W ) − H(U | V W ) = [H(U W ) − H(W )] − [H(U V W ) − H(V W )].

We also use H(U | W ) ≥ 0 and I(U ; V | W ) ≥ 0.
   Relevant to secret sharing are the additional consequences:

Lemma 1. Let A be unauthorised in a perfect scheme.

    1. If Aa is authorised, then
                                            H(a | A) ≥ H(S).

    2. If both Aa and Ab are authorised, where a ̸= b, then

                                            I(a; b | A) ≥ H(S).

Proof. For the first assertion,

                    H(a | A) ≥ I(S; a | A) = H(S | A) − H(S | Aa) = H(S).

Here the first inequality follows from I(S; a | A) = H(a | A) − H(a | S, A) ≤ H(a | A).
   For the second assertion, we expand both conditional mutual informations:

                         I(a; b | A) − I(a; b | A, S)
                                                      
                       = H(Aa) + H(Ab) − H(A) − H(Aab)
                                                              
                          − H(ASa) + H(ASb) − H(AS) − H(ASab)
                                                      
                       = H(AS) − H(A) − H(ASa) − H(Aa)
                                                              
                          − H(ASb) − H(Ab) + H(ASab) − H(Aab)
                       = H(S | A) − H(S | Aa) − H(S | Ab) + H(S | Aab).


                                                   2
Because A is unauthorised, perfect privacy gives H(S | A) = H(S). Because Aa and Ab are
authorised, correctness gives
                                H(S | Aa) = H(S | Ab) = 0.
Moreover, Aab is authorised by monotonicity, so H(S | Aab) = 0. Therefore
                                  I(a; b | A) − I(a; b | A, S) = H(S).
Finally, I(a; b | A, S) ≥ 0, and hence I(a; b | A) ≥ H(S).

5    Symmetries and orbit notation
For ε ∈ {+1, −1} and p, q ∈ Zk , define

                   Dε,p,q :     Xi 7→ Xεi+p ,         Yi 7→ Yεi+q ,     hs 7→ hεs+p−q ,           (D)
                   Wε,p,q :     Xi 7→ Yεi+p ,     Yi 7→ Xεi+q ,         hs 7→ h−εs−p+q .          (W)

    The inverse maps are
                               −1                            −1
                              Dε,p,q = Dε,−εp,−εq ,         Wε,p,q = Wε,−εq,−εp .

   We argue that these maps preserve authorisation. Indeed, Dε,p,q sends hs to hεs+p−q and
sends every edge
                                    {Yi , Xi+s } ∈ Ms
to an edge of Mεs+p−q . Similarly, Wε,p,q sends hs to h−εs−p+q and sends every edge of Ms to
an edge of M−εs−p+q . Consequently, if A contains hs and covers Ms , then g(A) contains the
corresponding helper and covers the corresponding matching, so g(A) is authorised. Applying
the same argument to g −1 gives the converse. Hence

                              A is authorised     ⇐⇒         g(A) is authorised

for every displayed map g; in particular, unauthorised coalitions are also preserved.
    For k ≥ 3, these 4k 2 maps are distinct: whether a map preserves or exchanges the X- and
Y -classes determines whether it is a D or a W ; the images of X0 , Y0 determine p, q; and the
image of X1 determines ε, since 1 ̸≡ −1 (mod k). They form a group G, as is also seen from

              Da,p,q Db,r,t = Dab,ar+p,at+q ,                   Da,p,q Wb,r,t = Wab,ar+q,at+p ,
              Wa,p,q Db,r,t = Wab,ar+p,at+q ,                   Wa,p,q Wb,r,t = Dab,ar+q,at+p .
