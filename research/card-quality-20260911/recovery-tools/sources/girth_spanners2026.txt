1     Introduction
1.1     Spanners and the Girth Conjecture
A multiplicative spanner is a primitive in graph theory and algorithms, which sparsifies an input
graph while approximately preserving its shortest path distances. Spanners were abstracted in the
late ’80s after being implicitly studied in networking, distributed algorithms, etc (see survey [1]).
Definition 1 (Spanners [29]). Given a graph G = (V, E, w), a subgraph H = (V, E ′ , w) is a
k-spanner of G if for all nodes s, t, we have distH (s, t) ≤ k · distG (s, t).
    Spanners and their variants are often studied from the extremal perspective, where the goal is
to prove that one can always construct a spanner with a good tradeoff between size and error. The
following classic theorem of Althöfer, Das, Dobkin, Joseph, and Soares [2] conditionally settles the
extremal tradeoff available for multiplicative spanners:
Theorem 2 ([2]). For all positive integers n, k, every n-node graph has a (2k − 1)-spanner on
O(n1+1/k ) edges. Moreover, assuming the girth conjecture [18], for all positive integers k there are
families of n-node graphs on which this size bound cannot be improved to o(n1+1/k ).
   The girth conjecture referenced in this theorem, attributed to Erdős [18], says the following. Let
us denote by ex(n, C≤2k ) the maximum possible number of edges in an n-node graph that does not
have any cycles of length ≤ 2k as subgraphs. There is a simple folklore counting argument, called
the Moore bound, that implies                               
                                    ex(n, C≤2k ) ≤ O n1+1/k .

The girth conjecture posits that this bound is asymptotically best possible for all k. The girth
conjecture is an old and very difficult problem in extremal graph theory, likely beyond the reach
of current techniques. It has been confirmed only for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} [34, 35], with no new cases
settled since the 1950s. In fact, the community has more recently started to regard it with some
skepticism. For example, in an article from 2021, Conlon [14] writes:
“It is now quite commonplace to believe that the true value of certain extremal numbers lie below
what the classical arguments give. We suspect that this should already be the case for C8 , that is,
                                   that ex(n, C8 ) = o(n5/4 ).”

Here ex(n, C8 ) is the maximum possible number of edges in an n-node graph with no C8 subgraph.
It follows from the definitions that ex(n, C8 ) ≥ ex(n, C≤8 ), and so the suggested bound of o(n5/4 )
would imply that the girth conjecture is false already for k = 4. Since this seems difficult to prove,
Conlon goes on to give a more attainable conjecture, very roughly stating that the techniques from
incidence geometry used to confirm the girth conjecture for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} cannot be extended
to other values of k. This conjecture is related to the classical Feit-Higman Theorem [19], which
is sometimes interpreted as further evidence against the girth conjecture.1 For further technical
discussion and references on the girth conjecture and its surrounding literature, we recommend the
excellent set of lecture notes by Szabó [33].
   1 The constructions implying the girth conjecture for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} arise from highly symmetric incidence struc-

tures known as finite generalized polygons. The Feit-Higman Theorem constrains the existence of finite generalized
polygons, and in particular, it implies that finite generalized polygons cannot be used to prove the girth conjecture
for any further values of k.

1.2     Fault Tolerant Spanners
Spanners are often used in distributed systems and networking, where there is a possibility of
nodes or edges in the input graph G temporarily failing and becoming unusable while they await
repair. A desirable feature in these application domains is for a spanner H to retain its distance
approximation guarantees even after any “reasonable” failure event occurs.
    The first formal model of fault tolerant spanners was introduced by Levcopoulos, Narasimhan,
and Smid [22]. They defined H to be an f -vertex (edge) fault tolerant k-spanner of G if, for
any set F of |F | ≤ f vertices (edges), the subgraph H \ F is still a k-spanner of G \ F . The
initial results in this model were for geometric graphs, which has remained an area of intensive
focus for the community [9–12, 15, 21–24, 32]. Results for general graphs were obtained later by
Chechik, Langberg, Peleg, and Roditty [13], which has inspired a similarly long line of followup
work [4–6, 8, 16, 17, 25]. By now, this model of fault tolerance is mostly well understood. We have
optimal existential bounds [4, 8] and near-optimal algorithms [25] for the setting of vertex faults.
We also have optimal existential bounds for edge faults for infinitely many values of spanner error
k [4, 5], although some knowledge gaps remain. As usual, the lower bounds in both settings are
conditioned on the girth conjecture.2
    More recently, the community has turned toward new and more expressive models of fault
tolerance, that strengthen the original model without harming the spanner size too much. One
such model is color-fault tolerant spanners, a compelling model of correlated failure introduced
recently by Petruschka, Sapir, and Tzalik [31] (see also [26, 27, 30, 31]). Optimal or near-optimal
bounds are known for this setting as well, again with the lower bounds conditioned on the girth
conjecture.3 Another more expressive fault tolerance model, which will be a focus of this work, is
degree fault tolerant spanners:

    An f -DFT spanner can tolerate many more edge failures at once than in the original model
of fault tolerance; for example, it can handle O(nf ) edge failures, so long as they are spread
throughout the graph such that at most f failing edges are incident to any particular node. Despite
this possibility of massive failure, Bodwin, Haeupler, and Parter [7] showed that fairly reasonable
existential size bounds are available, not much worse than the ones known for the original model:
Theorem 4 ([7]). For all positive integers k, every n-node graph G has an f -DFT (2k − 1)-spanner

which any f -DFT (2k − 1)-spanner H must have
                                                           
                               |E(H)| ≥ Ω f 1−1/k · n1+1/k .

   Thus conditionally nearly-tight bounds are known for this problem, up to a factor of exp(k),
and assuming the girth conjecture. Followup work by Parter and Tzalik [28] obtained matching
upper and lower bounds in the reachability (k = ∞) setting, and Biniaz, Carufel, Maheshwari, and
Smid [3] obtained results in the DFT model for metric and geometric spanners.

1.3     Our Results
The contribution of this paper is to show that the additional strength of the DFT model is enough
to obtain unconditional lower bounds. We show:
Theorem 5. For all positive integers k, there are n-node input graphs G for which any f -DFT
(2k − 1)-spanner H must have
                                                          
                               |E(H)| ≥ Ω f 1−1/k · n1+1/k .

    These lower bounds are both optimal up to Ok (1) factors, and they hold via explicit constructions
that do not assume the girth conjecture. This makes DFT spanners essentially the first variants
of multiplicative spanners where one can obtain unconditionally near-optimal lower bounds. For
essentially every other variant, the best unconditional lower bounds are inherited from the lower
bounds for the function ex(n, C≤2k ), where the state-of-the-art is from a 1995 work of Lazebnik,
Ustimenko, and Woldar [20]. These bounds are polynomially below the bound posited by the girth
conjecture, meaning that our results represent polynomial improvements in the known unconditional
bounds.
    These theorems may also have independent interest from the standpoint of extremal combina-
torics. In some sense, they state that the DFT model captures the gap between known algebraic
techniques and the structure that would be needed to prove the girth conjecture, if possible. More
specifically, our proofs analyze the Wenger graphs [35], which are an elegant algebraic construction
that notably achieves optimal lower bounds on the function ex(n, C2k ) for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5}.4 Szabó
   4 The Wenger graphs also imply the girth conjecture for k ∈ {1, 2, 3}, but not for k = 5: although they do not

[33] and Conlon [14] independently devised a reinterpretation of Wenger’s original construction with
a more algebraic flavor. We adopt their interpretation, and make algebraic arguments to reason

2     Proof of Main Result
2.1     Construction of Incidence System and Fault Sets
We begin by constructing an incidence system, consisting of n points and n lines (subsets of the
points). This construction and its incidence graph are originally by Wenger [35], while our algebraic
exposition follows Szabó [33] and Conlon [14].
    • (The parameters.) The construction is parametrized by an arbitrary prime p and an
      arbitrary integer k ≥ 1. We will have n := pk points and lines in the construction. We will
      use the standard shorthand [p] := {0, 1, . . . , p − 1}.
    • (The points.) The set of points A is the elements of the finite field Fkp . We interpret Fkp as
      a vector space with scalars in Fp , and so we will perform scalar multiplication with scalars
      from [p].
    • (The lines.) For any t ∈ [p], let σt := (1, t, t2 , . . . , tk−1 ) ∈ Fkp ; we will refer to σt as a slope
      vector. The set of lines L is exactly the lines of slope σt for any t ∈ [p]. In other words, a
      subset of points forms a line ℓ iff there exists t ∈ [p] such that ℓ is a maximal subset of points
      with the property that any two points in ℓ differ by a multiple of σt . Formally:
                              L := x + λσt | λ ∈ [p] | x ∈ Fkp , t ∈ [p] .
                                    


    • (The graph.) We define B to be the bipartite incidence graph between the set of points A
      and the set of lines L. That is: the left side is A, the right side is L, and there is an edge
      {a, ℓ} ∈ E(B) iff a ∈ ℓ.
    The graph B has the following statistics:
Lemma 6 (Size of B). The graph B has 2n nodes and n1+1/k edges.
Proof. There are n = pk nodes in Fkp . The lines are indexed by pk possible choices of x and p
possible choices of t, but each line can be generated in p ways (by choosing any of its p points for
x), so there are pk · p/p = pk = n lines. Thus B has n nodes on each side of its bipartition, for
2n nodes in total. Each line contains exactly p points, so it generates p edges in B. So there are
pk+1 = n(k+1)/k = n1+1/k edges in B.
   Our goal will be to argue that B is the only 1-DFT (2k − 1)-spanner of itself. This gives our
desired lower bound in the special case f = 1. Later, in Section 2.4, we will describe a standard
technique used to boost the lower bound to arbitrary f ≥ 1. The first step towards this goal is, for
every edge e, to define a corresponding edge set Fe of maximum degree 1 (i.e., a matching) such
that e does not participate in any cycles of length ≤ 2k in the graph B \ Fe . The next step in our
proof is to define these sets Fe . The following definitions will be useful:

2.4    Proof Wrapup
The following theorem certifies our lower bound for the special case f = 1:
Theorem 14. The only 1-DFT (2k − 1)-spanner of B is B itself.
Proof. Let H be a proper subgraph of B, and we will show that H is not a 1-DFT (2k − 1)-spanner
of B. Consider an edge
                                  e = {u, v} ∈ E(B) \ E(H).
    Let Fe denote the set associated to this edge (as in Definition 8). By Lemma 11, the set Fe has
maximum degree 1. Next, let π be a shortest u ⇝ v path in the graph H \ Fe . Since e ∈  / E(H), we
have e ∈/ π, and so π ∪ {e} is a cycle in B. This cycle contains e but it does not contain any edge
of Fe . By Lemma 13, this means π ∪ {e} contains > 2k edges. Thus π contains > 2k − 1 edges, so
we have
                                  |E(π)| = distH\Fe (u, v) > 2k − 1.
On the other hand, e ∈ / Fe , so we have distB\Fe (u, v) = 1. This implies that H is not a 1-DFT
(2k − 1)-spanner of B.
