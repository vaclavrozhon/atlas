                                                     Linear Discrepancy is Π2-Hard to Approximate

                                                                                              Abstract
                                                 In this note, we prove that the problem of computing the linear discrepancy of a given matrix is Π2 -
                                              hard, even to approximate within 9/8 − ǫ factor for any ǫ > 0. This strengthens the NP-hardness result
                                              of Li and Nikolov [LN20] for the exact version of the problem, and answers a question posed by them.
                                              Furthermore, since Li and Nikolov showed that the problem is contained in Π2 , our result makes linear
                                              discrepancy another natural problem that is Π2 -complete (to approximate).


                                        1 Introduction
                                        The linear discrepancy [LSV86] of a matrix A ∈ R m×n is defined as
                                                                            lindisc(A) := max          min kA(w − x)k∞ .
                                                                                          w ∈[0,1]n x ∈{0,1} n

                                            Besides its connection to combinatorial discrepancy and its variants [LSV86], the linear discrepancy
                                        also has applications in other areas, such as approximation algorithms (e.g. [EPR13, Rot16, HR17]); for
                                        example, the best known approximation algorithm for the bin packing problem [HR17] uses an algorithm
                                        for linear discrepancy of [LM15] as a subroutine. Although such an algorithm can (given A and w) find x
                                        that has “small” kA(x − w)k∞ , the guaranteed bound does not directly involve lindisc(A). Thus, it is not
                                        an (approximation) algorithm for computing lindisc(A) given A. Li and Nikolov [LN20] investigated the
                                        complexity of this problem; they showed that the problem belongs to Π2 and that it is NP-hard. Due to this,
                                        they asked whether the problem is Π2 -hard (which would mean that it is complete for Π2 ). We resolve this
                                        problem by showing that approximating linear discrepancy is Π2 -hard:
                                        Theorem 1. It is Π2 -hard to approximate the linear discrepancy of a given matrix within 98 − ǫ factor for any ǫ > 0.

                                        Other Related Works. The computational complexity of computing or approximating several notions of
                                        discrepancy has been investigated in recent years. The aforementioned work of Li and Nikolov [LN20] gave
                                        several algorithms for lindisc when m is small, and a polynomial time O(2n )-approximation algorithm in
                                        the general case. For the classic notion of (combinatorial) discrepancy, Charikar et al. [CNN11] showed that
                                        it is√NP-hard to distinguish even the case that the discrepancy is zero and the case where the discrepancy is
                                        Ω( m) when m = O(n); this is essentially
                                                                              √        the strongest possible hardness for the problem since the algo-
                                        rithms of [Ban10, LM15] ensures O( m) bound for any A with m = O(n). For the hereditary discrepancy,
                                        Matoušek et al. [MNT18] gave a polylogarithmic approximation algorithm for the problem while Austrin
                                        et al. [AGH17] showed that it is NP-hard to approximate beyond a factor of 2.
                                             As pointed out in [LN20], linear discrepancy is also related to the covering radius of a lattice, which can
                                        be defined in a similar manner as linear discrepancy except x is over all Z n (instead of {0, 1}n ). For this
                                        problem, Haviv and Regev [HR12] showed that it is Π2 -hard to approximate within some constant factor.
                                        Another related problem is that of computing the covering radius of a linear error correcting code; this
                                        problem is known to be Π2 -hard to approximate within some constant factor and NP-hard to approximate
                                        within Ω(log log n) factor [GMR05]. Since both of these problems belong to Π2 , they are examples of prob-
                                        lems which are Π2 -complete to approximate within some constant factor; with our main result, computing
                                        linear discrepancy now joins this class of problems.

3 Warm-up: NP-hardness of Approximating lindisc
In this section, we will prove a weaker version of Theorem 1 in which Π2 -hardness is relaxed to only NP-
hardness, as stated below. While this result is of course subsumed by Theorem 1, it demonstrates the main
new gadget required in our work, which will also be used for the Π2 -hardness result.
Theorem 2. It is NP-hard to approximate the linear discrepancy of a given matrix within 89 − ǫ factor for any ǫ > 0.
    Following [LN20], we reduce from the NAE3SAT problem where we are given a set V of variables and
a 3CNF formula φ over V. The goal is to determine whether there exists an assignment to V which makes
every clause of φ has at least one literal evaluated to true and at least one literal evaluated to false.
    We will now give an informal intuition for our proof. We will sometimes be vague; everything will be
formalized below. Our reduction builds on the reduction of [LN20], which works by viewing each clause
as a row vector of the matrix A in a natural manner, i.e., the i-th entry is 1 if the literal x i is present, -1 if the
literal ¬ xi is present and 0 otherwise. It is not hard to see that, when the starting instance is a NO instance
of the NAE3SATproblem, then lindisc(A, 0.5 · 1) ≥ 3/2. Similarly, in the YES case, lindisc(A, 0.5 · 1) ≤ 1/2.
The latter unfortunately is insufficient to conclude that lindisc(A) is small, as 0.5 · 1 may not be a deep hole
of A. Nonetheless, Li and Nikolov managed to use the fact that a deep hole has a polynomial bit complexity
to prove that lindisc(A, 0.5 · 1) ≤ 3/2 − exp(− poly(nm)) in the YES case. Thus, their result only implies
hardness of approximation with factor only 1 + exp(− poly(nm)).
    As one can see from the above outline, the most important challenge in the above reduction is in bound-
ing lindisc(A, w) for w 6= 0.5 · 1 in the YES case. Our main idea is to make multiple (specifically 3) copies
of each column. We then add gadgets on them in such a way that, when w = 0.5 · 1, the three columns are
forced to have the same value (and hence the NO case remains similar to before). In the YES case, we can
show that, while our gadget is restrictive when w = 0.5 · 1, it is “not as restrictive” for w far from 0.5 · 1,
which eventually allows us to overcome the previously challenging scenario.

3.1 Our Gadget
Our gadget is a simple (3 × 3) matrix G that can be used to enforce the three columns to be the same when
the target vector u is 0.5 · 1, which will be used in the NO (i.e. soundness) case. On the other hand, for any
target vector u (not necessarily equal to 0.5 · 1) and any sign b ∈ {−1, +1}, we can find a 0-1 vector z with
low discrepancy with respect to G while also maintaining that the sign of the sum of entries of u − z agrees
with b. And that such a sum does not have too large absolute value. We note that, in the reduction, the
sign b will be selected according to the assignment of the NAE3SAT instance, and that the sign agreement
together with the absolute value bound help ensure that the linear discrepancy is small in the YES case.

4 Proof of the Main Result: Π2 -hardness of Approximating lindisc
We will now prove our main result of the paper: Π2 -hardness of approximating lindisc. To do this, we
reduce from the Π2 -complete variant of NAE3SAT, called NAE∀∃3SAT. In the NAE∀∃3SAT problem, we
are given two sets VA , VE of variables and a 3CNF formula φ over VA ∪ VE . The goal is to determine whether,
for every assignment to VA , there exists an assignment to VE which makes every clause of 3CNF has at least
one literal evaluated to true and at least one literal evaluated to false. NAE∀∃3SAT is known to be Π2 -
complete [EG95]. The properties of our reduction are summarized below in Lemma 5, which immediately
implies our main theorem (Theorem 1).
Lemma 5. There exists a polynomial-time reduction that takes in an instance (VA , VE , φ) of NAE∀∃3SAT and
produces a matrix A′ such that the following holds.

5 Discussion and Open Questions
In this note, we prove that approximating lindisc to within a factor of 9/8 − ǫ is Π2 -hard. As stated above,
the best known polynomial time algorithm only gives an approximation ratio of O(2n ) [LN20]. It remains
an interesting open question to close this gap. Specifically, a concrete direction is to prove NP-hardness or
Π2 -hardness of approximation for all constant factors. For the latter, it should be noted that, for the related
problem of computing the covering radius of a lattice or a linear code, approximating it to within a factor
of 2 belongs to the class AM [GMR05] and is thus unlikely to be Π2 -hard. However, we are not aware of
any similar barrier for approximating linear discrepancy.
