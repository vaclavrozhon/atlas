                                                                                             Abstract
                                                   The Unique-Machine Precedence Scheduling (UMPS) problem, introduced by (Davies, Kulka-
                                               rni, Rothvoss, Sandeep, Tarnawski, and Zhang, 2022), seeks a makespan-minimizing schedule
                                               of precedence-constrained jobs when each job has a unique eligible machine. On the one hand,
                                               UMPS generalizes job shop scheduling by allowing the precedence graph to be an arbitrary
                                               DAG rather than a disjoint union of chains. On the other hand, UMPS admits approximation-
arXiv:2607.26590v1 [cs.DS] 29 Jul 2026




                                               preserving reductions to scheduling problems with communication delays, including the job-job
                                               delay model (DKRSTZ, 2022) and the job-machine delay model (Rajaraman, Stalfa, and Yang,
                                               2023). Despite its central role, the approximability of UMPS has remained poorly understood:
                                               even for unit-length jobs, known scheduling techniques do not seem to yield a non-trivial ap-
                                               proximation, and the existence of a polylogarithmic approximation was left open by (DKRSTZ,
                                               2022). On the hardness side, the previous best lower bound for unit-length jobs was only the
                                               5/4 inherited from job shop scheduling (Williamson, Hall, Hoogeveen, Hurkens, Lenstra, Sev-
                                               ast’janov, and Shmoys, 1997).
                                                   We prove that unit-length UMPS is NP-hard to approximate within any constant factor. We
                                               further show that, assuming NP is not in quasi-polynomial time, unit-length UMPS admits no
                                               polynomial-time (logn)γ -approximation for some constant γ > 0. Via the known reductions from
                                               UMPS, these lower bounds also transfer to the corresponding unit-length communication-delay
                                               scheduling models.
                                                   Our proof proceeds via a reduction from a hypergraph coloring promise problem. In the yes
                                               case, the input h-uniform hypergraph admits a k-coloring in which no color appears more than a
                                               times in any hyperedge; in the no case, the hypergraph has no large independent set. We convert
                                               such a promise into a UMPS gap of order h/a. Instantiating this framework with a balanced
                                               hypergraph coloring hardness of (Guruswami and Lee, 2018) gives arbitrary constant-factor
                                               inapproximability, while combining the 4-colorable 4-uniform hypergraph coloring hardness of
                                               (Guruswami, Harsha, Håstad, Srinivasan, and Varma, 2017) with a certain composition opera-
                                               tion for hypergraphs yields the polylogarithmic factor inapproximability.

1    Introduction
Precedence constraints are a classic source of computational difficulty in scheduling problems [LRK78,
GLLRK79]. Jobs form a directed acyclic graph: a job can start only after all its predecessors
have completed, and the makespan objective asks to complete the whole DAG as quickly as pos-
sible. This basic model has two intertwined sources of difficulty: the partial order among jobs
and the constraints governing machine use. Several classical variants enrich the latter: machine-
eligibility constraints restrict each job to a prescribed subset of machines [LL08, LLP11]; unrelated-
machine models let the processing time pij depend on both the job and the machine [LST90]; and
communication-delay models impose extra separation when dependent jobs are executed on different
machines [PY90].
    Unique-Machine Precedence Scheduling (UMPS) takes a complementary minimalist view of the
job-machine interaction: every job is assigned to a specific machine in advance, so the scheduler
can only choose the order of the jobs on each machine, subject to arbitrary precedence constraints
between jobs on possibly different machines. Thus UMPS asks whether precedence scheduling is
already hard before one uses assignment choices, machine-dependent processing times, or commu-
nication delays.
    UMPS lies between several better-known scheduling problems. On the upstream side, job shop
scheduling is a special case of UMPS, in which the precedence graph is a disjoint union of chains.
Therefore, UMPS inherits the hardness for job shop: quasi-polynomial-time logarithmic inapprox-
imability with arbitrary job lengths [MS11], and 5/4 NP-hardness when jobs have unit lengths
[WHH+ 97]. On the downstream side, in their paper introducing UMPS, Davies, Kulkarni, Rothvoss,
Sandeep, Tarnawski, and Zhang [DKR+ 22] reduce it to the non-uniform job-job communication-
delay problem, where jobs are assigned to identical machines, and each precedence edge j ≺ k has
a delay cj,k that must elapse between the completion of j and the start of k if the two jobs are
scheduled on different machines. The resulting instance has only unit-length jobs if UMPS is of
unit-length. Rajaraman, Stalfa, and Yang [RSY23] later considered a job-machine delay model, in
which the cross-machine delay is a function ρk,i of the successor job k and the machine i: if k starts
on machine i, then every predecessor j ≺ k scheduled on a different machine must complete at least
ρk,i time before k starts. They gave an approximation-preserving reduction from UMPS to this
job-machine delay model.
    The algorithmic picture of UMPS remains poorly understood. When the fixed-machine restric-
tion is absent, there are various approximation algorithms for scheduling with precedence, e.g.,
Graham’s list-scheduling 2-approximation on identical machines [Gra66], and O(log m/ log log m)
approximations for related machines [CS99, CB01, Li17]. For precedence scheduling with commu-
nication delays, positive results are known mainly under strong structural restrictions: uniform
delays admit polylogarithmic approximations via Sherali–Adams clustering and duplication-based
rounding [DKR+ 20, MRS+ 20], while certain additive non-uniform delay models admit polyloga-
rithmic guarantees [RSY23]. These algorithms, however, rely on the flexibility of assigning jobs to
suitable machines, which UMPS does not offer. Therefore, they do not yield comparable guarantees
for UMPS itself. Indeed, the work introducing UMPS explicitly points out that classical job-shop
algorithms and recent LP-hierarchy methods fail to provide non-trivial approximation guarantees
for UMPS [DKR+ 22]. This led them to formulate the following strong hardness conjecture, even
for the unit-length case:
Conjecture 1.1 ([DKR+ 22], Conjecture 2). There exists an absolute constant ε > 0 such that it
is NP-hard to approximate UMPS with n jobs within a factor of nε , even when all jobs have unit
length.

                                                 1
    In this work, we prove that unit-length UMPS is NP-hard to approximate within any con-
stant factor. Moreover, assuming NP ⊈ DTIME(npolylog(n) ), it does not admit a polynomial-time
(log N )γ -approximation for some constant γ > 0, where N denotes the instance size. These results
substantially strengthen the previous 5/4 inapproximability for UMPS inherited from job shop
scheduling, and mark a step toward proving Conjecture 1.1.

Theorem 1.2. For any constant γ > 1, γ-approximating unit-length UMPS is NP-hard.

Theorem 1.3. Assume NP ⊈ DTIME(npolylog(n) ). For some constant γ > 0, unit-length UMPS
has no polynomial-time (log N )γ -approximation, where N is the instance size.

    Our proof proceeds via reduction from a hard form of hypergraph coloring. Given an h-uniform
hypergraph G, we rely on the following hardness promise: in the yes case, G admits a k-coloring in
which no color appears more than a < h times in any hyperedge; in the no case, no independent
set contains more than an ε < 1/k 2 fraction of vertices. By carefully designing vertex and hyper-
edge machine gadgets to encode the coloring, we show that any hardness of this form yields an
inapproximability factor of O(h/a) for unit-length UMPS. We then instantiate our reduction with
the hardness result in [GL18], where h/a can be made an arbitrarily large constant, thereby prov-
ing Theorem 1.2. We also analyze how the hypergraph composition method in [EL75] affects the
parameters relevant to our reduction, and thus amplify the gap to (log N )Ω(1) , proving Theorem 1.3.
    As corollaries, the reductions in [DKR+ 22] and [RSY23] transfer our lower bounds to two non-
uniform communication-delay scheduling problems on identical machines with unit-length jobs: the
job-job delay model, where a precedence edge j ≺ k has a delay cj,k incurred when j, k run on
different machines, and the job-machine delay model, where if job k is scheduled on machine i,
every predecessor of k scheduled on different machines must finish at least ρk,i time before k starts.
For each of these two problems, there is no polynomial-time constant-factor approximation unless
P = NP; and under NP ⊈ DTIME(npolylog(n) ), there is no polynomial-time (log N )γ approximation
for some constant γ > 0, where N is the instance size.
    Alongside our main construction, we give a simple PCP-free reduction showing that unit-length
UMPS is NP-hard to approximate within a factor of 4/3. This already improves the previous
5/4 inapproximability inherited from unit-length job shop scheduling, and may be of independent
interest. We include it in Section A. We also found a simple and clean reduction from (2 + ε)-SAT
[AGH17] that gives factor 4/3 NP-hardness of unit-length UMPS, albeit it is not PCP-free. We
omit the details of this reduction.

1.1   Proof overview
We first explain the reduction in a minimal setting where all parameters are concrete: yes instances
have a schedule of makespan 12, whereas no instances have makespan at least 13. This is not yet a
large approximation gap, but it contains the main ideas of the full construction in Section 3.
    We use the following hypergraph coloring hardness as the starting point: for every fixed ε > 0,
it is NP-hard to distinguish a 2-colorable 4-uniform hypergraph H = (V, E) from one in which
every independent set has size less than ε|V |. Here we take ε < 1/2, and the parameters are
k = 2, h = 4, a = 3, since no hyperedge is monochromatic means each color appears on at most 3
vertices of every hyperedge. This hardness follows from the verifier of Guruswami–Håstad–Sudan,
together with the independent-set strengthening of Holmerin; see [GHS02, Hol02]. In this subsection,
we call the two colors 0 and 1, and index the time starting at 0.

2     Preliminaries
Definition 2.1 (The Unique-Machine Precedence Scheduling (UMPS) Problem). An instance of
the unique-machine precedence scheduling problem consists of a set of machines M, a set of jobs
J , a precedence relation ≺ forming a directed acyclic graph, a map σ : J → M assigning every job
to its unique eligible machine, and a function ℓ : J → R+ specifying the processing time of each
job. A feasible schedule assigns each job j a starting time s(j) such that

    1. s(j) ≥ 0,

    2. jobs assigned to the same machine do not overlap, and

    3. j ≺ j ′ implies s(j) + ℓ(j) ≤ s(j ′ ).

    The objective is to minimize the makespan

                                          Cmax = max{s(j) + ℓ(j)}.
                                                 j∈J

Definition 2.2 (a-Bounded k-Coloring of Hypergraphs). An a-bounded k-coloring, abbreviated as
an (a, k)-coloring, of a hypergraph G = (V, E) is a mapping χ : V → [k], such that for every e ∈ E
and r ∈ [k],
                                         |e ∩ χ−1 (r)| ≤ a.

   This notion is also called an a-proper k-coloring in [BM25]. For an h-uniform hypergraph, the
canonical notion of k-coloring is equivalent to the (h − 1)-bounded k-coloring defined here.

Definition 2.3 (The (h, a, k, ε)-Balanced Coloring Problem). Fix integers h, k ≥ 2, 1 ≤ a < h, and
real 0 < ε ≤ 1/k. (h, a, k, ε)-Balanced Coloring asks to distinguish between the following two cases
for an h-uniform hypergraph H = (V, E):

    • (Yes) H is (a, k)-colorable.

    • (No) Any independent set in H is of size < ε · |V |.


3     A Reduction from Hypergraph Coloring to Unit-Length UMPS
Theorem 3.1. Fix integers h, k ≥ 2, 1 ≤ a < h, and real 0 < ε ≤ 1/k. There is a polynomial-time
reduction from an (h, a, k, ε)-Balanced Coloring instance H to a unit-length UMPS instance Γ, such
that

    • In the Yes case, OPT(Γ) ≤ (k + 2c)a.

                                                       4
   • In the No case, OPT(Γ) ≥ ka + c(a + h).

    It is useful to think of having k virtual copies H (1) , . . . , H (k) of the input hypergraph H = (V, E).
In the yes case, there is a balanced coloring of H, and thus we can make a cyclic shift on the coloring
of H (1) , . . . , H (k) , such that the k copies of each vertex are assigned k different colors. In the no
case, even if we only consider a partial coloring, where for each vertex, one of its k copies is assigned
a fixed color (say color k) while the others are left uncolored, in some hypergraph copy the set of
colored vertices is too large to stay independent, yielding a monochromatic hyperedge.
    The physical machines are indexed by a layer t ∈ {0, . . . , c}.

Corollary A.2. For every constant ε > 0, unit-length UMPS is NP-hard to approximate within a
factor 4/3 − ε.

Proof. Let ρ < 4/3 be fixed, and choose k such that ρ < (4k + 1)/(3k + 1). By Theorem A.1, it is
NP-hard to distinguish OPT ≤ 3k + 1 from OPT ≥ 4k + 1. A ρ-approximation would distinguish
the two cases because ρ(3k + 1) < 4k + 1.
