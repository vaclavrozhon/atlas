                                             Systems on Graphs with Girth At Least Five

                                                                     Xiaoyu Chen∗                 Kuikui Liu†

                                                                                      Abstract
                                                   It is proved that, for every δ ∈ (0, 1), the Glauber dynamics for the uniform
                                               distribution on proper q-colorings is rapidly mixing when q ≥ (1 + δ)∆ and the
                                               underlying graph has girth at least 5 and maximum degree ∆ = Ωδ (1). This
                                               result also extends to general multi-spin systems satisfying a local spectral contraction
                                               condition, including the anti-ferromagnetic Potts model with q ≥ (1 + δ)(1 − β)∆.
                                               These results are achieved by a new spectral local-to-global principle on graphs with
                                               girth at least five for general multi-spin systems, and a novel Fourier analysis for
                                               Glauber dynamics on a star. The main ideas behind all the proofs were developed
                                               through several rounds of interaction with GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra.

1    Introduction
Sampling from the uniform distribution µ on proper q-colorings is a fundamental
problem in randomized algorithms, probability, and statistical physics. Despite decades
of progress, efficient sampling when q is close to the maximum degree ∆ remains a
major open problem. One of the simplest and most well-studied samplers is the (heat-
bath) Glauber dynamics, which proceeds by iterating the following two-step procedure:
choose a vertex uniformly and recolor it uniformly from the colors absent from its
neighborhood. This Markov chain is reversible with stationary distribution µ, and
ergodic when q ≥ ∆ + 2.
    A long-standing folklore conjecture states that Glauber dynamics mixes rapidly
throughout the range q ≥ ∆ + 2 [Jer95]. For general graphs of maximum degree ∆,
Jerrum proved optimal O(n log n) mixing for Glauber dynamics when q > 2∆ [Jer95].
The current state-of-the-art result is due to Carlson–Vigoda [CV25], who established
rapid mixing of a Markov chain known as the flip dynamics when q > C∆ for C ≈ 1.809,
building upon prior advances by Vigoda [Vig00] and Chen–Delcourt–Moitra–Perarnau–
Postle [CDM+ 19].
    Stronger results are known when the graph is locally sparse. Let α∗ ≃ 1.763 be the
positive solution of α∗ = exp(1/α∗ ). Dyer and Frieze, Hayes, and Molloy developed
burn-in and local-uniformity methods for large-girth graphs [DF03, Hay03, Mol04].
Dyer, Frieze, Hayes, and Vigoda subsequently proved optimal O(n log n) mixing of
the Glauber dynamics, for every fixed ε > 0 and all sufficiently large ∆, when either
q > (α∗ + ε)∆ and the girth is at least five, or q > (1.489 + ε)∆ and the girth is at least
seven [DFHV13]. Spectral-independence arguments later reached the α∗ threshold
on all triangle-free graphs [CGŠV21, FGYZ22]; subsequent work obtained an almost-
optimal spectral-gap bound [JPV22] and then an optimal order-1/n spectral gap [CF25]
in this regime.
    The regime closest to the conjectured threshold has so far required additional girth.
Hayes and Vigoda introduced a non-Markovian coupling targeting the range q ≥
(1 + δ)∆ at girth at least eleven in the large-degree setting ∆ = Ω(log n) [HV03]. Jain,
Mizgerd, and Vigoda recently resolved the technical obstacles in this framework and
extended it to all sufficiently large constant degrees, proving optimal Oδ (n log n) mixing
of the Metropolis chain [JMV26]. In a different direction, Chen, Liu, Mani, and Moitra
proved optimal Glauber mixing for fixed ∆ and q ≥ ∆ + 3 when the girth is sufficiently
large as a function of ∆ [CLMM23].

Remark 6. The abstract framework (Theorem 20 and Theorem 25) is sufficiently general

models, in a fixed-gap ∆-ary uniqueness regime. Earlier rapid-mixing results used
Weitz’s self-avoiding-walk tree [Wei06] to reach the corresponding uniqueness regime
for the (∆ − 1)-ary tree recursion; see, for example, [ALO20, CLV23, CFYZ24, CCYZ25].
For fixed positive uniqueness slack, the distinction between the ∆-ary and (∆ − 1)-ary
regimes is asymptotically negligible as ∆ → ∞. It may also be of independent interest
that our method entirely avoids the self-avoiding-walk tree and thereby sidesteps the ob-
stacles to extending Weitz-style reductions to multispin systems identified by [LMP24].

1.2    Discussions about experiments with AI
This work is mainly inspired by the recent work of Göbel, Jenssen, Michelen, Pappik,
Perkins, and Schiller [GJM+ 26], which gives a direct Bochner–Bakry–Émery proof
of the spectral-gap criterion of Chen, Chen, Chen, Yin, and Zhang [CCC+ 25]. The
original proof of this criterion proceeds through a trickle-down theorem for field dy-
namics [CCC+ 25].
    We first asked GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra to use the Bochner identity to simplify the
proofs of several results obtained by trickle-down methods, including Oppenheim’s
trickle-down theorem [Opp18, AL20], spectral independence via the pairwise
spectral-influence matrix [LO25], and the matrix trickle-down theorem for sampling
edge-colorings [ALO21]1 .

Remark 7 (Discussion of the current obstruction). The existence of spanning 4-cycles
breaks both the local-to-global argument in Section 3 and the local analysis in Section 4.
We asked GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra to work on triangle-free graphs with the Bochner identity,
but it did not find a proof after ≥ 20 hours.
   In fact, spanning 4-cycles seem to be the only remaining hard gadgets for coloring.
Given the present manuscript, GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra claims that it has found a proof that
generalizes Theorem 1 to all graphs without spanning 4-cycles. The proof follows a high-
level plan similar to the one presented in this manuscript. For the local expansion, it uses
the fact that, without spanning 4-cycles, the induced subgraph on the neighbors of any
vertex is a matching. However, this makes the analysis of the local gadget significantly
more sophisticated. We omit this result from this manuscript since we did not find any
new ideas in it.

1.3   Related work
Rapid mixing for the anti-ferromagnetic Potts model. The zero-temperature case
β = 0 is the coloring model surveyed above. At positive temperature, early work used
Dobrushin-type and spatial-mixing arguments to establish rapid Glauber mixing, with
subsequent refinements for bounded-degree graphs and lattices [SS97, GJMP06]. Later
work developed correlation-decay and zero-free approaches to approximate count-
ing [YZ15, BBR26, LSS25], clarified uniqueness and spatial mixing on trees [GGY18,
dBBR23, BdBBR23, CLMM23, BBR25], and obtained polynomial-time sampling on ran-
dom regular and sparse random graphs [BGG+ 20, YZ16, Eft22]. Complementary
hardness and slow-mixing results describe the obstructions below the natural scale
q = (1 − β)∆ and at low temperature [GŠV15, LLY26].

Trickle-down methods. Beginning with Garland’s method, local-to-global spectral
arguments propagate expansion from links to high-dimensional walks; this lineage
now includes local spectral expansion, spectral and entropic independence, and scalar,
matrix, partite, poset, localization, and C -Lorentzian trickle-down theorems [Gar73,

                                             6
BŚ97, Żuk03, PR17, KM17, DK17, Par17, Opp18, KO20, Opp20, ALOV19, CGM21, AL20,
ALO20, GM20, FGYZ22, JPV22, CLV21, ALO21, AJK+ 22, KT26, AO23, GK23, AKV24,
CCC+ 25, LLO25, LO25]. Our local-to-global theorem follows the same philosophy,
but it uses an exact operator expansion and the girth-five geometry to collect every
interacting pair of updates inside a closed star.

Bochner-type inequalities. Beginning with Bochner’s identity and Bakry–Émery
theory, comparisons between a Dirichlet form and its iterated form have been used to
derive Poincaré and entropy-decay inequalities [Boc46, BÉ85, BGL14]. Discrete versions
were developed for finite Markov chains and interacting-particle systems [BCDP06,
CP07, Cap08, CDP09, DP13, Maa11, EM12, Mie13, EMT15, FM16, EHMT17, EF18], with
more recent extensions involving Beckner and curvature-dimension inequalities and
coupling-based curvature bounds [JY17, WZ21, Ped25]. Related squared-generator
expansions have also been applied to spectral-gap estimates for hard-core and anti-
ferromagnetic two-spin models [KKO13, GJM+ 26, GZ26]. Our Bochner identity follows
this line, using the girth-five geometry to localize pairwise update terms on closed stars.
