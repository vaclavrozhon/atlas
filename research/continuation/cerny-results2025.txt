                           1. Concepts and methods
1.1. Basic definitions. A complete deterministic finite automaton (a DFA,
for short) is a triple A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩, where Q and Σ are finite non-empty sets
and δ : Q × Σ → Q is the transition function. The elements of Q and Σ are
referred to as states and (input) letters, respectively, so Q is the state set
and Σ is the input alphabet of A . Automata are understood to evolve in
discrete time: at each moment, a DFA A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ is in a certain state
q ∈ Q. During the next time unit, exactly one input letter a ∈ Σ arrives,
causing A to transition to the state δ(q, a).
   A DFA A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ can be represented by a labeled directed graph with
the vertex set Q, the label alphabet Σ, and the set of edges
                       a
                      → q ′ | q, q ′ ∈ Q, a ∈ Σ, δ(q, a) = q ′ }.
                   {q −
Thus, the transition from state q to state q ′ under input a is represented by
an edge from q to q ′ labeled by a. The underlying graph of A is obtained by
omitting the labels from this graph representation. A DFA is called strongly
connected if its underlying graph is strongly connected.
   A word over an alphabet Σ is any finite sequence of letters from Σ. The
set of all words over Σ, including the empty word ε, is denoted by Σ∗ . If
w = a1 · · · aℓ with a1 , . . . , aℓ ∈ Σ is a non-empty word, the number ℓ is said
to be the length of w and is denoted by |w|. The length of the empty word is
defined to be 0. Words are multiplied by concatenation, and the empty word
serves as the identity element for this multiplication. Thus, Σ∗ is a monoid.
   The transition function δ of ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ extends to a function Q × Σ∗ → Q
(still denoted by δ) in the following way. For each q ∈ Q, let δ(q, ε) := q and
if w = a1 · · · aℓ with a1 , . . . , aℓ ∈ Σ is a non-empty word, let
                    δ(q, w) := δ(. . . δ(δ(q, a1 ), a2 ), . . . , aℓ ).
   For any DFA A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩, every word w ∈ Σ∗ induces the transforma-
tion q 7→ δ(q, w) on the set Q. The set of all transformations induced this
way is closed under the composition of transformations and contains the
identity transformation (induced by the empty word). Thus, it constitutes
a monoid called the transition monoid of A .

   When a DFA is fixed, one often simplifies the notation by suppressing the
transition function symbol. That is, the DFA is specified as a pair ⟨Q, Σ⟩,
and one writes q . w instead of δ(q, w).
1.2. Synchronizing automata and the Černý conjecture. A DFA A =
⟨Q, Σ⟩ is called synchronizing if there exist a word w ∈ Σ∗ and a state s ∈ Q
such that q . w = s for all q ∈ Q. Such a word w is said to reset A to the
state s and is called a reset word for A . The minimum length of reset words
for A is called its reset threshold and denoted by rt(A ).
   Synchronizing automata are the subject of a rich theory with diverse con-
nections and a wide range of applications; see [39] or [76] for an introduc-
tion and overview. Much research on synchronizing automata per se centers
around the question of how the reset threshold of a synchronizing automaton
relates to its number of states. The results collected in Section 2 belong to
this line of research.
   The Černý conjecture asserts that the reset threshold of every synchroniz-
ing automaton with n states does not exceed (n − 1)2 . See [76, Section 3.1]
for the history of the conjecture, which dates back to the 1960s. As of the
time of composing this version of the list of results (January 14, 2026), the
Černý conjecture remains neither confirmed nor refuted1.
   Jan Černý [21] introduced a series of synchronizing automata {Cn }n=2,3,... .
The automaton Cn has n states, 2 input letters, and reset threshold (n − 1)2 .
The states of Cn are the residues modulo n, on which the input letters a
and b act as follows:
      0 . a := 1, m . a := m for 0 < m < n, m . b := m + 1 (mod n).
Fig. 1 shows a generic automaton from the Černý series2. Černý has proved
that the shortest reset word for Cn is the word (abn−1 )n−2 a of length (n−1)2 .
   The Černý function C(n) is defined as the maximum reset threshold of
all synchronizing automata with n states. The above property of the series
{Cn } yields the inequality C(n) ≥ (n − 1)2 . The Černý conjecture amounts
to the claim that in fact the equality C(n) = (n − 1)2 holds.
   As of September 2025, not only the truth of the equality C(n) = (n − 1)2 ,
but also the existence of any upper bound on C(n) that is quadratic in n,
remain unknown. The best upper bound known to date is the following cubic
one, due to Yaroslav Shitov [57]:

researchers have considered its restrictions to certain special classes of au-
tomata. If S is a class of DFAs, the Černý function CS (n) restricted to S
is defined as the maximum reset threshold of all n-state synchronizing au-
tomata from S. The results surveyed in Section 2 aimed to determine, or at
least upper-bound, the function CS (n) for various classes S.

1.3. Methods. Let A = ⟨Q, Σ⟩ be a DFA, P a non-empty subset of Q, and
v a word over Σ. The image and the preimage of P under v are defined as
         P . v := {p . v | p ∈ P } and P v −1 := {q ∈ Q | q . v ∈ P },
respectively. With this notation, the fact that a word w ∈ Σ∗ resets A to
a state s ∈ Q can be expressed by either of the equalities Q . w = {s} or
{s}w−1 = Q. These equalities indicate two possible directions in construct-
ing reset words: compression (top-down) and extension (bottom-up).

    The top-down approach starts with the set Q of all states and applies
words u1 , u2 , u3 , . . . such that u1 shrinks Q, u2 shrinks the image Q . u1 ,
u3 shrinks the image Q . u1 u2 , and so on, until the image Q . u1 u2 · · · um
becomes a singleton, and hence, w := u1 u2 · · · um is a reset word. For general
synchronizing automata, greedy compression, in which each next word ui is
chosen to be the shortest possible word that strictly shrinks the preceding
image, yields reset words whose lengths witness the Pin–Frankl bound.
    The bottom-up approach begins with a state s ∈ Q and seeks words
v1 , v2 , v3 , . . . such that the size of {s}v1−1 is greater than 1, the size of
({s}v1−1 )v2−1 is greater than that of {s}v1−1 , and so on, until the preimage
under vm of (. . . (({s}v1−1 )v2−1 ) . . . )vm−1

                                                 is all of Q. Then w := vm · · · v2 v1
is a reset word.
    Given a positive constant α, a DFA with n states is said to be α-extensible
if for each proper non-singleton subset P of its states, there is a word v
of length at most αn such that |P v −1 | > |P |. The following observation
was stated (without proof) as Proposition 2.3 in [25], with a reference to
an unpublished manuscript by Savický and Vaněček; for a proof, see [76],
where the result appears as Proposition 3.4. A similar observation was made
in [50, Theorem 2].

Proposition 1. Each α-extensible DFA with n > 2 states is synchronizing,
and its reset threshold does not exceed 1+αn(n−2). In particular, the Černý
conjecture holds for 1-extensible automata.

   Arguments using induction on the number of states often involve the con-
cepts of subautomata and quotients.
   A subautomaton of a DFA ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ is any DFA of the form ⟨S, Σ, τ ⟩,
where non-empty S ⊆ Q satisfies δ(s, a) ∈ S for all s ∈ S and a ∈ Σ, and
τ : S × Σ → S is the restriction of δ to S × Σ.
   A congruence on a DFA A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ is an equivalence relation π
on the set Q such that, for all p, q ∈ Q and a ∈ Σ, if (p, q) ∈ π then
 δ(p, a), δ(q, a) ∈ π. The quotient A /π is the DFA ⟨Q/π, Σ, δπ ⟩, where Q/π
is the set of all π-classes [q]π for q ∈ Q, and the function δπ : Q/π ×Σ → Q/π
is defined by δπ ([q]π , a) := [δ(q, a)]π .
   A DFA is an automaton with zero if it contains a state that is fixed by
every letter. The following reduction is sometimes useful.

Proposition 2 ([76, Lemma 3.3]). Let S be any class of automata closed
under taking subautomata and quotients, and let S0 and SSC be the classes
of automata with zero from S and strongly connected automata from S, re-
spectively. Let f : Z+ → N be a function such that

(1)            f (n) ≥ f (n − m + 1) + f (m) for all n ≥ m ≥ 1.

If CS0 (n) ≤ f (n) and CSSC (n) ≤ f (n), then CS (n) ≤ f (n).

A1† . Circular automata
   • Definition: A DFA is called circular if one of its letters acts as a cyclic
permutation of the entire set of states.
   • Upper bound: (n − 1)2 ; see [26].
   • Method: Circular synchronizing automata are 1-extensible [26, Propo-
sition 4.6], so Proposition 1 applies.
   • Lower bound: (n − 1)2 , since the automata Cn from the Černý series
are circular.
   • Comment: For a circular synchronizing automaton with n states, greedy
compression yields a reset word of length at most n2 ln n [36, Theorem 13].
   • Version stamp: August 2025 update (comment added).

A4† . Automata respecting the intervals of a directed graph
   • Definition: Let ∆ be a directed graph whose vertex set is the state
set of a DFA A = ⟨Q, Σ⟩. (Warning: in general, ∆ is different from the
underlying graph of A , although the two graphs share the same vertex set.)
For p, r ∈ Q, a path from p to r in ∆ is called singular if neither p nor r
occurs on this path as an intermediate vertex. Let [p, r] := ∅ if ∆ has no
path from p to r, and otherwise let
            [p, r] := {q ∈ Q | q lies on a singular path from p to r}.
The graph ∆ is dense if, for all p, q, r in the same strongly connected com-
ponent of ∆, either q ∈ [p, r] or q ∈ [r, p].
   The DFA A respects the intervals of the graph ∆ if the following hold for
all p, r ∈ Q and every a ∈ Σ:
       - if [p, r] ̸= ∅, then [p . a, r . a] ̸= ∅,
       - if [p, r] ̸= ∅ and [r, p] ̸= ∅, then [p, r] . a ⊆ [p . a, r . a],
       - if p . a = r . a, then at least one of [p, r] . a or [r, p] . a is a singleton.
Class A4 consists of strongly connected automata respecting the intervals of
some weakly connected dense graph on their state sets.
  • Upper bound: (n − 1)2 , see [33, Corollary 4.1].

   • Method: Induction on the number of states combined with a case-by-
case analysis.
   • Lower bound: (n − 1)2 , since the automata Cn from the Černý series lie
in A4. (The DFA Cn is strongly connected and respects the intervals of the
cycle 0 → 1 → 2 → · · · → n − 1 → 0, which is a dense graph.)

A5† . 2-junction automata
    • Definition: A DFA with the input alphabet Σ is a 2-junction automaton
if for every letter a ∈ Σ, one of the following conditions holds:
      - all states not fixed by a, except for at most two, are fixed by every
        letter from Σ \ {a}, and each of the exceptional states is not fixed by
        exactly one letter from Σ \ {a},
      - all states not fixed by a, except for one, are fixed by every letter from
        Σ \ {a}, and the exceptional state is not fixed by exactly two letters
        from Σ \ {a}.
  • Upper bound: (n − 1)2 , see [34, Theorem 2.1].
  • Method: Reduction to DFAs in classes A1 and A6 and an analysis of
the remaining cases.
  • Lower bound: (n − 1)2 , as the Černý automata Cn lie in A5.

   • Definition: Let A = ⟨Q, Σ⟩ be a DFA and let a word w ∈ Σ∗ be such

C5† . Media
    • Definition: In a DFA ⟨Q, Σ⟩, two letters a, b ∈ Σ are reverses of each
other if, for any distinct states p, r ∈ Q, one has p . a = r if and only if
r . b = p. A word in Σ∗ is consistent if it does not contain two letters that
are reverses of each other. A word w ∈ Σ∗ is vacuous if each letter a ∈ Σ
occurs in w the same number of times as does the reverse of a. A word
a1 · · · aℓ , where a1 , . . . , aℓ ∈ Σ, is stepwise effective for a state q ∈ Q if
q . a1 ̸= q и q . a1 · · · ai ̸= q . a1 · · · ai−1 for all i = 2, . . . , ℓ. A medium is a
DFA ⟨Q, Σ⟩ satisfying the following axioms:
         - every letter has a unique reverse,
         - for any distinct states p, r ∈ Q, there exists a consistent word w ∈ Σ∗
           such that p . w = r,
         - if a word w ∈ Σ∗ is stepwise effective for a state q ∈ Q, then q . w = q
           if and only if w is vacuous,
         - if p . u = r . v, where u and v are consistent words such that u is
           stepwise effective for p and v is stepwise effective for r, then the
           word uv is consistent.
    • Upper bound: n − 1, see [28, Theorem 1].
    • Method: Reduction to a search for a shortest path in an acyclic auxiliary
graph on the vertex set Q.
    • Lower bound: The lower bound n − 1 is claimed in [28], although an
example of a medium with reset threshold n − 1 is is provided only for n = 6.

Lemma 1 ([46, Theorem 2.1.1]). If n, k are coprime positive integers, then
nk − n − k is the largest integer that is not expressible as a non-negative
integer combination of n and k.

A10. Binary DFAs with a simple idempotent letter: Upper bound
    Let A = ⟨Q, {a, b}⟩ be a synchronizing automaton such that |Q| = n and
the letter a is a simple idempotent. We prove that A has a reset word of
length at most (n − 1)2 .
    Let e stand for the only state in Q \ Q . a. We say that a state q ∈ Q is
b-cyclic if q = q . bℓ for some positive integer ℓ. A b-cycle of A is any set of
states of the form {q . bk | q is a b-cyclic state, k is a nonnegative integer}.
The letter b acts as a cyclic permutation on each b-cycle.
    If a b-cycle C excludes the state e, then C ⊆ Q . a whence C . a = C as a
fixes every state in Q . a. Besides, C . b = C as b acts as a cyclic permutation
on C. Hence, C . w = C for every word w ∈ {a, b}∗ . On the other hand,
letting w be a reset word for A , we get |C . w| = 1. We see that C must
consist of just one state z such that z . a = z . b = z so z is a zero of A .
