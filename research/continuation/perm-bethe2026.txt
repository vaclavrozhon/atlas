We keep the information lost in both steps. This gives an exact decomposition of Δ(𝐴) into three
nonnegative contributions: the suboptimality of 𝑃 in the Bethe program, the sum of the deficits in the
one-row inequality, and the average relative entropy between the true and sequential distributions.
Consequently, when Δ(𝐴) is small, all three contributions are small.
The one-row inequality is stable: a row with small deficit must be close to a probability vector
supported uniformly on two columns. Hence most rows of 𝑃 have two entries near 1/2. These large
entries form a bipartite graph of maximum degree two. The relative-entropy contribution rules out
many long cycles in this graph, so most rows lie in approximate copies of 𝐾 2,2 , the complete bipartite
graph on two rows and two columns. This is the rigidity statement behind the improvement.

Transfer the blocks and collect their gain. The marginal matrix 𝑃 is not available to the algorithm.
We optimize a slightly regularized Bethe objective instead, adding a small multiple of the sum of
the Shannon entropies of its rows. The unique optimizer 𝑋 is positive. Its Karush–Kuhn–Tucker
optimality equations give positive row and column scaling factors 𝑟 𝑖 , 𝑐 𝑗 such that

                                       𝐴 𝑖𝑗 = 𝑟 𝑖 𝑐 𝑗 𝑋𝑖𝑗1+𝜏 (1 − 𝑋𝑖𝑗 ).

Here 𝜏 is a small positive regularization parameter. A global identity derived from this factorization
measures how much the relevant coordinates can change between 𝑃 and 𝑋. It shows that most of
the approximate 𝐾2,2 ’s found in 𝑃 remain useful at 𝑋. On each such block, the paired certificate
improves on the two singleton certificates by a constant factor. A maximum-weight row matching
collects these gains on a linear number of disjoint blocks, improving the lower bound by 𝑒 Ω(𝑛) .
The key point is the interaction between the two sides of the argument. The stability analysis
identifies precisely where the Bethe bound can be nearly tight, while the paired certificate is
designed to recover weight on those same configurations.

1.2   Related work
The Bethe permanent was introduced and analyzed systematically by Vontobel [Von13]. Its lower-
bound property follows from Schrĳver’s inequality and Gurvits’s variational formulation [Sch98;
Gur11]. Anari and Rezaei proved the matching 2𝑛/2 upper bound [AR24]. Our proof begins with
their sequential-distribution argument but uses its discarded relative-entropy term as a structural
statistic.
Graph covers give another interpretation of the Bethe permanent. Ng and Vontobel studied the role
of double covers [NV22]; Huang, Kashyap, and Vontobel developed degree-𝑀 Bethe and Sinkhorn
permanent bounds [HKV24]. These works analyze the Bethe approximation and its finite-cover
counterparts. Our certificate instead recovers weight that the Bethe objective necessarily loses on
2 × 2 blocks.
The stable-polynomial input is the multiaffine coefficient inequality of Anari and Oveis Gharan
[AO17]. It proves one-sidedness of each paired certificate; the global argument deciding which
pairs are profitable is information-theoretic.
