                                                                                             Abstract

                                                        In 1989, Erdős and Hajnal conjectured that for any graph H, there is a constant c =
                                                    c(H) > 0 such that every n-vertex graph G with no induced copies of H contains a clique
                                                    or an independent set of size at least nc . This conjecture, known as the Erdős-Hajnal
                                                    conjecture, is a central open problem in combinatorics and listed as one of the top 10 Erdős
                                                    problems by Bloom on the Erdős problem website https://www.erdosproblems.com/. In
                                                    a recent breakthrough, Nguyen, Scott and Seymour proved that Erdős-Hajnal conjecture
                                                    holds for the case when H is the five-vertex path, which, combined with known results,
                                                    implies that Erdős-Hajnal conjecture holds for every five-vertex graph.
                                                        In this paper, we extend the iterative sparsification framework recently developed by
                                                    Nguyen, Scott and Seymour. We introduce a generalized niceness condition relaxing their
                                                    nice condition, a novel intermediate property concerning combs and a general structural
                                                    lemma (which may be of independent interest) that is sufficient to deduce the Erdős-Hajnal
                                                    conjecture.
                                                        This framework simultaneously recovers the recent result on the five-vertex path (PLMS
                                                    2026) and the classical result on the bull graph by Chudnovsky and Safra (JCTB 2008) as
                                                    special cases, thereby unifying these two previously independent strands, and further proves
                                                    the conjecture for two new cases: the E-graph (which contains the five-vertex path) and the
                                                    Bird graph (which contains both the five-vertex path and the bull). These are the first two
                                                    six-vertex graphs whose validity does not follow from the known operations (see Alon-Pach-
                                                    Solymosi, Combinatorica 2001, and Nguyen-Scott-Seymour, TAMS 2026) that preserve the
                                                    Erdős-Hajnal property.



                                           1       Introduction

                                           A clique (stable set) is a vertex set whose vertices are pairwise adjacent (nonadjacent, resp.).
                                           Ramsey [17] proved that for every integer k ≥ 1, there exists a positive integer R(k, k) such
                                           that every graph on at least R(k, k) vertices contains a clique or stable set of size k. This shows
                                           that a sufficiently large graph must contain a large clique or stable set. Erdős [9] proved via
                                           probabilistic method that for large n, there exists an n-vertex graph with no cliques or stable


                                                                                                 1
sets of size at least 2 log n. This shows that general graphs do not nacessarily contain a clique
or stable set of polynomial size. However, H-free graphs might behave differently.
   Let G, H be graphs. We say that G is H-free if G has no induced subgraph that is isomorphic
to H. In 1989, Erdős and Hajnal [10] showed that for any graph H, there exists a constant c =
                                                                                           √
c(H) > 0 such that any n-vertex H-free graph has a clique or stable set of size at least ec log n .
Erdős and Hajnal [10] furhter conjectured that one can improve the bound to a polynomial of
n, which has become known as the Erdős-Hajnal conjecture.

Conjecture 1. (Erdős-Hajnal [10]) For every graph H, there exists c = c(H) > 0 such that
every n-vertex H-free graph has a clique or stable set of size at least nc .

    In 2024, Bucić, Nguyen, Scott and Seymour [5] gave an log log improvement over the classical
Erdős-Hajnal bound: for any graph H, there exists a constant c = c(H) > 0 such that any n-
                                                                    √
vertex H-free graph has a clique or stable set of size at least ec log n log log n . For a set H of
graphs, G is H-free if G is H-free for all H ∈ H. The Erdős-Hajnal conjecture is equivalent
to the statement that for any nonempty set H of graphs, there exists a constant c = c(H) > 0
such that any n-vertex H-free graph has a clique or stable set of size nc . If a nonempty set H
of graphs satisfies this statement, we say that H has the Erdős-Hajnal property. In this case,
We call c(H) a Erdős-Hajnal constant for H. It is clear that a graph H has the Erdős-Hajnal
property if and only if H has the Erdős-Hajnal property, where H is the complement of H.


1.1   Equivalent formulations

The Rödl theorem says that every graph in a proper hereditary graph class contains an induced
subgraph with linear size that is very sparse or very dense. Formally, we say that X is c-sparse
if every vertex in X has at most c|X| neighbors in X, and X is c-restricted if X or X is c-sparse.

Theorem 1.1. (Rödl [18]) For every graph H and every ϵ ∈ (0, 12 ), there exists δ > 0 such that
every H-free graph G has an ϵ-restricted induced subgraph of size at least δ|G|.

    The original proof of Rödl uses the regularity lemma which gave a tower-type bound
on δ. Fox and Sudakov [12] made a significant improvement showing that one can take
               1 2
δ = e−c|H|(log ϵ ) for some constant c > 0, and they conjectured that one can take δ to be
a polynomial in ϵ. A set H of graphs (or the class of H-free graphs) has the polynomial Rödl
property, if there exists d ≥ 1 such that for every ϵ ∈ (0, 21 ), every H-free graph G has an
ϵ-restricted induced subgraph of size at least ϵd |G|. By taking ϵ to be a small polynomial of
|G|−1 , one may see that the polynomial Rödl property implies the Erdős-Hajnal property.
   For two graphs G and H, an induced copy of H in G, is an injection ϕ : V (H) → V (G) such
that for all u, v ∈ V (H), uv ∈ E(H) if and only if ϕ(u)ϕ(v) ∈ E(G). The number of induced
copies of H in G is denoted by indH (G). Nikiforov generalized Theorem 1.1 to the graphs that
contain few induced copies of H.

Theorem 1.2. (Nikiforov [16]) For every graph H and every ϵ ∈ (0, 12 ), there exists δ > 0 such
that every graph G with indH (G) < (δ|G|)|H| has an ϵ-restricted induced subgraph of size at least
δ|G|.

    A finite set H of graphs (or the class of H-free graphs) is viral, if there exists d ≥ 1 such that
every graph G with indH (G) < (ϵd |G|)|H| for all H ∈ H has an ϵ-restricted induced subgraph
of size at least ϵd |G|. Bucić, Fox and Pham [4] proved the equivalence of the Erdős-Hajnal
property, the polynomial Rödl property, and the viral property.

Theorem 1.3. (Bucić-Fox-Pham [4]) For a finite set H of graphs, H has the Erdős-Hajnal
property if and only if it has the polynomial Rödl property if and only if it is viral.


1.2   Erdős-Hajnal for a single graph

A vertex set S ⊆ V (G) is homogeneous in G if all vertices in S have the same neighborhood in
V (G) \ S. A homogeneous set S of G is nontrivial if 2 ≤ |S| ≤ |G| − 1. A graph G is prime if
G has no nontrivial homogeneous set.
   Let G1 and G2 be graphs with disjoint vertex sets, and a ∈ V (G1 ). We say that G is
obtained from G1 by substituting G2 for a if:
   • V (G) = (V (G1 ) \ {a}) ∪ V (G2 ),
   • u, v ∈ V (G1 ) \ {a} are adjacent in G if and only if u, v are adjacent in G1 ,
   • u, v ∈ V (G2 ) are adjacent in G if and only if u, v are adjacent in G2 ,
   • u ∈ V (G1 ) \ {a} and v ∈ V (G2 ) are adjacent in G if and only if u is adjacent to a in G1 .
    It is clear that a graph G on at least two vertices is prime if and only if there do not exist
graphs G1 , G2 with |G1 |, |G2 | ≥ 2 such that G is obtained from G1 by substituting G2 for a
vertex in G1 .
   One important result due to Alon, Pach and Solymosi [1] says that it suffices to consider
Conjecture 1 for prime graphs.

Theorem 1.4. (Alon-Pach-Solymosi [1]) Let H1 , H2 be graphs, and H be the graph obtained
from H1 by substituting H2 for a vertex v in H1 . If both H1 , H2 have the Erdős-Hajnal property,
then H also has the Erdős-Hajnal property.

1.3    Erdős-Hajnal for two graphs

In the following we mention some results on Erdős-Hajnal property of graphs with more than
one forbidden induced subgraph. Bousquet, Lagoutte and Thomassé [3] proved that for every
integer k ≥ 1, {Pk , Pk } has the Erdős-Hajnal property. A stronger result that if H1 , H2 are
forests, then {H1 , H2 } has the Erdős-Hajnal property [7] was proved by Chudnovsky, Scott,
Seymour and Spirkl. In the paper which proved that C5 has the Erdős-Hajnal property [8],
the authors gave a generalized result. Let H be a graph with vertex set {b1 , . . . , bk }. The
star-expansion of H, is the graph obtained from H that adds k + 1 vertices a1 , . . . , ak , v, where
ai is adjacent to bi for i ∈ [k], and v is adjacent to a1 , . . . , ak , and there are no other edges (see
Figure 3 for an example). They proved the following theorem.

bull graph, Theorem 1.11 generalizes the result of Chudnovsky and Safra [6]. In fact, as we

   While the extension from five-vertex graphs to six-vertex graphs may appear incremental,
the technical obstacles are substantial: the existing framework of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour
does not directly apply to these cases, and new structural tools-a generalized niceness condition
and a comb-based criterion-are required.
    Our proofs are inspired by [15]. The proof of Theorems 1.10 and 1.11 proceeds in four main
steps. First, we extend the framework of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour [15] by introducing a
generalized niceness condition that relaxes the original notion of ”nice”, and show that any
finite class of graphs satisfying this property, along with two technical conditions called leaf-
reducibility and wonderfulness (see Section 2 for precise definitions), has the Erdős-Hajnal
property (see Section 3).

Lemma 1.12. Let F be a finite class of graphs that is leaf-reducible and wonderful. If F is
generalized nice, then F has the Erdős-Hajnal property.

graph with one side having s vertices and other side having t vertices. If s = 1, the graph K1,t

H, let H = {H : H ∈ H}. For a graph G, we say G is connected (anti-connected) if for every
two vertices v1 , v2 ∈ G, there is a path in G (G, resp.) with ends v1 , v2 . We say S is a connected
component (anti-connected component) of G, if S is a maximal connected (anti-connected, resp.)

X is pure to Y if X is complete or anti-complete to Y , and X and Y are mixed otherwise. If
X = {x}, we say that x is mixed on Y .

X and Y are weakly c-sparse if |E(X, Y )| ≤ c|X||Y |. For convenience, we say that X and Y
are c-sparse if X is c-sparse to Y and Y is c-sparse to X. An (ℓ, w)-blockade in a graph G, is a
sequence B = (B1 , . . . , Bk ) of disjoint subsets of V (G), where k ≥ ℓ and |Bi | ≥ w for all i ∈ [k].

weakly x-sparse, resp.). We say B = (B1 , . . . , Bk ) is x-sparse if Bi is x-sparse to Bj for all
i, j ∈ [k] with i > j.

6    Completing the proof

In this section, we prove that co-E-free graphs and co-Bird-free graphs satisfy the hypothesis
of Lemma 5.1, which implies that E-graph and Bird satisfy the Erdős-Hajnal property. We
first define the quotient blockade of a blockade and derive some useful properties of quotient
blockades.
     Suppose that L = (L1 , . . . , Ln ) is a blockade. We say Li and Lj have relation M if and only
if i = j or there is a block sequence Li = Lr1 , Lr2 , . . . , Lrm = Lj such that for each k ∈ [m − 1],
Lrk and Lrk+1 are mixed. It is easy to check that M is an equivalence relation. Let L/M be
the quotient blockade of L where each block of L/M is the union of all blocks in an equivalence
class of M. We present two properties of the quotient blockade.

Lemma 6.1. Suppose that L is a blockade such that each block of L is connected (anti-
connected). Then

 (1) Each block of L/M is also connected (resp. anti-connected).

 (2) Each two blocks of L that are contained in different blocks of L/M are pure to each other.

 (3) If there are a block D of L/M and a vertex u ∈ / D that is mixed on D but is pure to each
     block of L contained in D, then there are two mixed blocks of L contained in D such that
     u is complete to one of the blocks and anti-complete to the other.

                                                         25
Proof. The first two statements of Lemma 6.1 follow immediately from the definition of L/M.
We now prove (3). Since u is mixed on D but is pure to each block of L contained in D, there
are two blocks of L, say A1 , A2 , that are contained in D such that u is complete to A1 and
anti-complete to A2 . Since D is an equivalence class of L with respect to M, there is a block
sequence A1 = Ar1 , Ar2 , . . . , Arm = A2 such that for each k ∈ [m − 1], Ark and Ark+1 are mixed.
So there is an index k such that u is complete to Ark and anti-complete to Ark+1 . This proves
Lemma 6.1 (3).                                                                                   □

connected). If there are two mixed blocks D1 , D2 of L/M and three vertices x, y, u ∈
                                                                                    / D 1 ∪ D2
such that

   • x, y are two non-adjacent vertices that are complete to D1 ∪ D2 ,

   • u ∈ N (x) \ N (y) is complete to D1 but is anti-complete to D2 , and

   • no vertex in D1 can mix on D2 ,

then there are two mixed blocks A1 , A2 of L that contained in D1 and three vertices x′ , y ′ , u′ ∈
                                                                                                   /
A1 ∪ A2 such that

 (1) x′ , y ′ are two non-adjacent vertices that are complete to A1 ∪ A2 and

 (2) u′ ∈ N (x′ ) \ N (y ′ ) is complete to A1 but is anti-complete to A2 .

Proof. Since D1 and D2 are mixed, either there is a vertex b1 ∈ D1 mixed on D2 or there is a
vertex b2 ∈ D2 mixed on D1 . By our assumption, there is a vertex b2 ∈ D2 mixed on D1 . By

7    Concluding remarks

In this paper, we combine several new ideas to extend the iterative sparsification framework
recently developed by Nguyen, Scott and Seymour [15]. Our framework not only yields the first
two six-vertex graphs satisfying the Erdős-Hajnal conjecture which do not follow directly from
Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.8, but also provides a general criterion (Lemma 1.14) that may
be used to verify the conjecture for further graph classes. Moreover, we recover the result of
Chudnovsky and Safra [6] on bull graph and the result of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour [15] on
P5 in a unified way. This suggests that the comb-based approach is a promising direction for
future work on the Erdős-Hajnal conjecture.
