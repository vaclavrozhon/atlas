                                                   Transversal non-Clifford gates on almost-good
                                                 quantum LDPC and quantum locally testable codes
                                                                           Yiming Li, Zimu Li, and Zi-Wen Liu

                                                             Yau Mathematical Sciences Center, Tsinghua University

                                                                                               Abstract
arXiv:2604.01874v2 [quant-ph] 2 Jul 2026




                                                     We exhibit nontrivial transversal logical multi-controlled-Z gates on [[N, Θ(N ), Θ̃(N )]] quantum
                                                 low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes with soundness Θ̃(1), combining nearly optimal code
                                                 parameters with fault-tolerant non-Clifford gates on qLDPC and quantum locally testable codes for
                                                 the first time. Remarkably, our proofs proceed through highly general algebraic arguments. Building
                                                 on insights from [Li et al., arXiv:2603.25831], we develop a general covering space framework for
                                                 constructing and computing a rich family of cohomological invariant forms on sheaf codes that
                                                 induce transversal logical multi-controlled-Z. To certify their nontriviality, we further demonstrate
                                                 the existence of two-way product-expanding punctured Reed–Solomon codes, which is striking in
                                                 light of the many negative examples for the product expansion behavior of ordinary Reed–Solomon
                                                 codes. This approach directly overcomes the previous obstruction to realizing nontrivial logical
                                                 operations while simultaneously preserving the code parameters. The claimed almost-good code
                                                 results follow immediately as examples.

1      Introduction and main results
Constructing quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes with desired properties is a deep and
central problem in quantum computation that has attracted sustained effort over many years. This pur-
suit carries both practical importance as a pathway to efficient fault-tolerant quantum computation [1–
5], as well as profound theoretical relevance through substantive connections with mathematics [6–10],
physics [11–13], and complexity theory [14–16]. A strong notion known as quantum locally testable
codes (qLTCs) [17] further involves a “soundness” property and has important motivations for e.g. im-
proving the efficiency of quantum fault tolerance [5] and the quantum probabilistically checkable
proofs (qPCP) conjecture [14, 15, 17, 18], a central problem in quantum complexity. In particular, qLTC
constructions with good distance and soundness were shown to imply the NLTS theorem [15], a major
landmark towards qPCP, prior to its independent resolution via qLDPC codes [16]. Long-term work
has recently culminated in breakthroughs which established “good” qLDPC codes with asymptotically
optimal code distance and rate [19–22] and “almost-good” qLTCs whose distance and soundness are
polylogarithmically away from optimal [18].
    Beyond basic code parameters, the logical operation properties are an essential consideration,
since quantum computation requires efficient dynamical processing of logical quantum information.
Specifically, we would like the codes to directly support fault-tolerant (e.g., transversal) physical im-
plementations of logical gates, especially non-Clifford gates, since they dominate the cost of universal
fault-tolerant quantum computation. Therefore, the ideal goal is to combine fault-tolerant non-Clifford
logical gates with desirable code parameters, although this has proven persistently difficult and its
very possibility has remained in doubt, with progress so far being limited despite extensive efforts
from diverse directions. First, there are fundamental no-go theorems that rule out certain general
combinations of qLDPC code and non-Clifford gate structures [23, 24]. In terms of existing progress,
non-Clifford gates have only been established in either i) qLDPC codes with parameters at least
polynomially below optimal [25–40], or ii) quantum (algebraic geometry) codes with good parameters
yet are non-LDPC [41–47]. Namely, while any two of the three desired properties—qLDPC, near-
optimal parameters, and fault-tolerant non-Clifford gates—have been achieved, it remains unknown
how, or indeed whether, all three can be realized simultaneously. Within the existing framework of
almost-good qLDPC codes and qLTCs on high-dimensional expanders and sheaves [18–21, 48–53],
the manifest algebraic complexity of the code space poses formidable challenges for analyzing the
structure of logical gates. A recent work [10] proposes a systematic methodology for formulating
cohomology invariants, including logical representatives and cup products, and presents explicit
computation of nontrivial non-Clifford operations on such sheaf codes, but the related results there
involve extra assumptions from number theory.

Main results. In this work, building on the cohomological invariant forms on sheaf codes proposed in
Ref. [9], we realize nontrivial transversal logical multi-controlled-Z gates on almost-good qLDPC codes
and qLTCs [18]. To this end, we demonstrate the existence of two-way product-expanding punctured Reed–
Solomon local codes that support nontrivial cup products in cohomology [10]. This resolves the open
problem of achieving fault-tolerant non-Clifford gates simultaneously with nearly optimal parameters
on qLDPC codes and qLTCs.
   As is standard, Cr−1 Z denotes a (multi-)controlled-Z gate on r qubits with r − 1 controls.

Theorem 1.1. For any integer r ≥ 2, there exist

    • [[N, Θ(N ), Θ(N/(log N )r−1 )]] quantum LDPC codes1

    • [[N, Θ(N ), Θ(N/(log N )2r−1 )]] quantum locally testable codes with soundness Θ(1/(log N )2r−1 )

that support nontrivial transversal logical Cr−1 Z gates.

set of k-dimensional cells in X, and call an element σ ∈ X(k) a k-cell. For σ, τ ∈ X with σ ≤ τ and

and less than σ in the cell poset, respectively.
    In this paper, we restrict attention to cell complexes whose cellular incidence numbers are either
±1 or 0, or equivalently, such that a k-cell is incident to any given (k − 1)-cell at most once. This
further implies that for any fixed k-cell σ and (k + 2)-cell π with σ < π, there exist an even number of
(k + 1)-cells τ such that σ ⋖ τ ⋖ π. Although all results can be established without this assumption, for
simplicity, one can regard this property as part of the definition.
    Although a finite cell poset is a purely combinatorial object, it admits a nontrivial topology, which
will play a key role in this paper.
Definition 2.3 (Alexandrov topology). Given a poset X, the Alexandrov topology of X is the topology
generated by the basis
                                      B := {X≥σ : σ ∈ X},                                      (2.5)
i.e., a subset U ⊆ X is open if and only if it is a union of sets in B.

2.2     Sheaves and their operations
Throughout the paper, Fq is a finite field of characteristic 2, and all the vector spaces are finite
dimensional over Fq . This is for the sake of finding high dimensional product-expanding local codes
in Section 4 (see also [54, 59, 60]). Since q is a power of 2, the final results can be pulled back to F2 and
realized in the system of qubits [43, 61].

Definition 2.6 (Sheaf). Let X be a poset with Alexandrov topology. A sheaf F on X is a functor from
X to vector spaces over Fq , i.e., it assigns to each element σ a vector space Fσ , and to each σ ≤ σ ′ a
linear map Fσ,σ′ : Fσ → Fσ′ such that for all σ ≤ σ ′ ≤ σ ′′ , we have Fσ,σ′′ = Fσ′ ,σ′′ ◦ Fσ,σ′ .

Definition 2.7 (Pullback sheaf). Let X, Y be two posets with Alexandrov topology, and f : X → Y
is an order-preserving map. Suppose G is a sheaf on Y , then the pullback sheaf f ∗ G on X is a sheaf
defined by, for each σ ∈ X,

                                               (f ∗ G)σ := Gf (σ) ,                                     (2.6)

and when σ ≤ σ ′ ∈ X, the restriction map is

                                           (f ∗ G)σ,σ′ := Gf (σ),f (σ′ ) .                              (2.7)

Definition 2.8 (Tensor product of sheaves). Let X be a poset equipped with two sheaves F and G, the
tensor product of the two sheaves F ⊗ G is defined by, for each σ ∈ X,

                                            (F ⊗ G)σ := Fσ ⊗ Gσ ,                                       (2.8)

and when σ ≤ σ ′ ∈ X, the restriction map is

                                        (F ⊗ G)σ,σ′ := Fσ,σ′ ⊗ Gσ,σ′ .                                  (2.9)

Definition 2.9 (External tensor product of sheaves). Let X and Y be posets with sheaves F and G
respectively. Let pX : X × Y → X and pY : X × Y → Y be the projection maps. The external tensor
product of F and G is the sheaf on X × Y defined by

                                           F ⊠ G := p∗X F ⊗ p∗Y G.                                     (2.10)

3    Cohomological invariant forms on sheaf codes
Cup and cap products are fundamental operations on (co)chain complexes in the construction of coho-
mological invariant forms: the Leibniz rules ensure that they are well-defined on (co)homology classes.
Moreover, the natural pairing between chains and cochains provides the basic linear cohomological
invariant form, which can be extended to multi-linear forms by using cup and cap products. Our
transversal logical Cr−1 Z gates on almost-good qLDPC codes and qLTCs are induced from invariant
forms built in this way.

Definition 3.5 (Subdivision functor). The subdivision functor sd is a functor from cell posets to simplicial
complexes. For each cell poset X, sd X is defined to be the simplicial complex called the barycentric
subdivision of X, whose vertices correspond to the cells of X, and whose k-simplices [σ0 , σ1 , · · · , σk ]
are the strictly increasing chains of cells in X.

                                                σ0 < σ1 < · · · < σk ∈ X.                                                 (3.15)

                                                    sd f : sd X → sd Y                                                    (3.16)

is defined to be a map given by, for each k-cell [σ0 , σ1 , · · · , σk ] ∈ sd X,

                                    (sd f )([σ0 , . . . , σk ]) := [f (σ0 ), . . . , f (σk )].                            (3.17)

It is straightforward to verify that sd f is order-preserving.

Definition 3.6 (Carrier map). Suppose X is a cell poset, the carrier map s : sd X → X is defined by, for
each k-cell [σ0 , σ1 , · · · , σk ] ∈ sd X,

[σ0 , σ1 , · · · , σk ] ∈ sd X.

Lemma 4.6. Let M ⊆ [n]3 be ϵ-closed. Then the following properties hold:

   1. Any cross section of M is ϵ-closed in 2 dimensions.

   2. The set of full lines L(M ) and M ′ := M \ L(M ) are ϵ-closed, and so are their cross sections.

   3. Within L(M ), any sectional graph of full lines is ϵ-closed.

   4. The set

                         Lcross (M ) := {(a, b, c) : intersection points of two lines in Lsparse (M )}   (4.68)

      is 2ϵ-closed. More than two lines may intersect at the same point, but such cases are encompassed by the
      definition above. Moreover, Lcross (M ) ∪ Ldense (M ) is 3ϵ-closed.

Proof. The first property has been used above. Formally, let S ⊆ M be any cross section, and let ℓ be
any line in the corresponding hyperplane.

                                  |ℓ ∩ S| ≥ ϵn =⇒ |ℓ ∩ M | ≥ ϵn =⇒ ℓ ⊆ M.                                (4.69)

Since ℓ is in the hyperplane that contains S, ℓ ⊆ S.
   Similarly,

                       |ℓ ∩ L(M )| ≥ ϵn =⇒ |ℓ ∩ M | ≥ ϵn =⇒ ℓ ⊆ M =⇒ ℓ ⊆ L(M ).                          (4.70)

For M ′ , similarly,

                 |ℓ ∩ M ′ | ≥ ϵn =⇒ |ℓ ∩ M | ≥ ϵn =⇒ ℓ ⊆ M =⇒ ℓ ⊈ M ′ = M \ L(M )                        (4.71)

   The sectional graph Gi is contained in any cross section S normal to the i-th direction. Then

                                          |ℓ ∩ Gi | ≥ ϵn =⇒ ℓ ⊆ S ⊆ M.                                   (4.72)

Thus ℓ lies in the dense part of Gi as its intersection with the sparse part cannot ≥ ϵn.
    It is important to note that not every subset of M is ϵ-closed. For the last case, suppose ℓ ∩
Lcross (M ) ̸= ∅. Since these intersection points lie on lines in sparse positions, in any fixed direction
there can be at most ϵn − 1 parallel sparse lines, and the total number cannot exceed 2ϵn − 2 < 2ϵn.
For Lcross (M ) ∪ Ldense (M ), the line ℓ can intersect Ldense (M ) along its direction. Summing these
contributions yields the result.

5     Transversal non-Clifford gates on almost-good qLDPC codes and
      qLTCs
We are now going to prove that the cup and cap products defined in Section 3 combining with the GRS
codes in Section 4 induce nontrivial multi-controlled-Z gates on almost-good qLDPC codes and qLTCs.
The key observation underlying our proof is that the almost-good qLTCs constructed in Ref. [18] are
closely related to a class of sheaved HGP codes [10]. To be precise, the base spaces and sheaves of
these codes arise as covering spaces and pullback sheaves of the corresponding HGP codes, on which
it is straightforward to compute cup and cap products. Furthermore, the two-way product-expanding
punctured RS codes can help preserve code parameters [18, 54] and ensure nontrivial cup and cap
products [10]. We now elucidate the construction, thereby establishing transversal non-Clifford gates
on almost-good qLDPC codes and qLTCs. This answers Conjecture 1.2 in Ref. [9] in the affirmative.

6    Discussion and outlook
Although the transversal non-Clifford gates on almost-good qLDPC codes and qLTCs are presented as
main results, we highlight that they emerge as an implication of highly general algebraic reasoning.
Beyond the specific cases, our derivation points to a broader principle: given a quantum code defined


                                                                    42
from a cell complex, with or without sheaf structures, fault-tolerant multi-controlled-Z gates can be
systematically constructed by cohomological invariant forms in a flexible manner. Looking ahead,
should good qLTCs eventually be constructed from cell complexes and sheaves as one may expect, our
framework should apply with little additional effort. This leads to the expectation that such codes
would likewise admit transversal logical multi-controlled-Z gates.
    An immediate next goal is to consider more intricate features of the logical operations, such as
addressability and parallelizability, for which we expect that significantly refined analysis and ideas
will be needed. Here, we have only rigorously proved the occurrence of nontrivial logical Cr−1 Z action,
that is, the subrank kCr−1 Z of the invariant form, which characterizes the number of parallelizable
logical gates, does not vanish. Establishing better lower bounds on kCr−1 Z remains open. One apparent
limitation of our current construction is that one of the code blocks involved is only known to possess
a constant number of logical qubits. Progress toward sharper estimates will likely first require a more
refined strategy for evaluating the rates of sheaf codes in Ref. [18] which facilitates a broader choice of
local codes in order to overcome the constant-logical-qubit bottleneck. Recent work [70] realizes Tanner
codes with constant rate by local codes of rate ≤ 12 . This breaks the low-local-rate barrier for achieving
a nontrivial global rate, but the construction is non-LDPC because the graph node degree and local
code length grow sublinearly as a fractional power. Nevertheless, it remains important to study the
low-local-rate regime and extend these results to high-dimensional sheaf codes. Subsequently, a deeper
understanding of specific logical representatives in the code space would be important, as they make
the expression of cohomological invariants, such as cup products, fully explicit [10].
    Furthermore, our constructions open the possibility of further improving the asymptotic spacetime
overhead of quantum fault tolerance, which we leave for separate work.
