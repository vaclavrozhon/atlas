France Abstract We show that, for every k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness can be decided in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds in the Broadcast CONGEST model, by a deterministic algorithm. This (deterministic) round-complexity is optimal for k = 2 k=2 up to logarithmic factors thanks to the lower bound for C 4 C_{4} -freeness by Drucker et al. [PODC 2014], which holds even for randomized algorithms. Moreover it matches the round-complexity of the best known randomized algorithms by Censor-Hillel et al. [DISC 2020] for k ∈ { 3 , 4 , 5 } k\in\{3,4,5\} , and by Fraigniaud et al. [PODC 2024] for k ≥ 6 k\geq 6 . Our algorithm uses parallel BFS-explorations with deterministic selections of the set of paths that are forwarded at each round, in a way similar to what is done for the detection of odd-length cycles, by Korhonen and Rybicki [OPODIS 2017]. However, the key element in the design and analysis of our algorithm is a new combinatorial result bounding the “local density” of graphs without 2 ​ k 2k -cycles, which we believe is interesting on its own. 1 Introduction In the context of distributed computing in networks, deciding H H -freeness for a given (connected) graph H H has attracted a lot of attention in the standard CONGEST model (see, e.g., the survey [ 2 ] ). Indeed, this problem is inherently local, and thus the main concern is measuring the amount of information that must be exchanged between the nodes for solving it. Recall that the CONGEST model [ 10 ] assumes n n processing nodes connected as an n n -node graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) . Computation proceeds as a sequence of rounds. During each round, every node can send an O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) -bit message to each of its neighbors, receive the messages sent by its neighbors, and perform some individual computation. In the broadcast version of the model, which is the one used in this paper, it is required that, at each round, each node sends the same message to all its neighbors. An algorithm decides H H -freeness if, for every graph G G , the following holds: If G G contains a subgraph isomorphic to H H then at least one node rejects , otherwise all nodes accept . For every k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , let C k C_{k} denotes the k k -node cycle. It is known that, for every k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , there exists a deterministic algorithm deciding C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} -freeness in O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) rounds [ 8 ] , which is optimal up to logarithmic factors. It is however possible to decide the presence of even-size cycles in a sub-linear number of rounds. In particular, there exists a deterministic algorithm deciding C 4 C_{4} -freeness in O ⁡ ( n ) O(\sqrt{n}) rounds [ 4 ] , which is optimal up to logarithmic factors, even for randomized algorithms. For every k > 2 k>2 , there exist randomized algorithms deciding C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds [ 3 , 6 ] . The algorithms in [ 3 ] , based on the “local threshold” technique, apply to 2 ≤ k ≤ 5 2\leq k\leq 5 , whereas the algorithms in [ 6 ] , based on the “global threshold” technique, apply to all k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . All aforementioned randomized algorithms are 1-sided, i.e., if G G contains a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle, then the probability that at least one node rejects is at least 2 / 3 \nicefrac{{2}}{{3}} , but if G G does not contain a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle, then the probability that all nodes accept is 1. Of course, the error probability of these algorithms can be made as small as desired by mere repetition. Yet, it may still be the case that G G contains a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle that is not detected, even if this occurs with arbitrarily small probability. This raises the question of whether C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness can be decided by a deterministic algorithm (which would thus provide absolute success) without increasing the round complexity. This is the case for all odd cycles of length ≥ 5 \geq 5 , and for 4-cycles [ 4 , 8 ] . We show that this holds for all even cycles as well, by establishing the following result. Theorem 1 . For every k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , there is a deterministic algorithm solving C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds in the Broadcast CONGEST model. Our deterministic algorithm solving C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds is generic, parameterized by k k . For k = 2 k=2 , i.e., for C 4 C_{4} -freeness, our algorithm coincides with the one in [ 4 ] . In fact, our algorithm can be viewed as a generalisation of the latter. Specifically, as for the algorithms in [ 3 , 6 ] , the algorithm distinguishes the case of light cycles, i.e., 2 ​ k 2k -cycles containing solely nodes with degree smaller than n 1 / k n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}} , from the case of heavy cycles, i.e., 2 ​ k 2k -cycles containing at least one node with degree at least n 1 / k n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}} . Light cycles can easily be detected deterministically by flooding, in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds [ 3 , 6 ] , and the main issue is to show that heavy cycles can also be be detected deterministically in O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) rounds. We show that this is indeed possible. Our algorithm (for detecting heavy cycles) relies on combining the Representative Lemma by Monien [ 9 ] , with a new “Density Theorem”. In a nutshell, the Representative Lemma can be used to show that, as already observed in [ 7 , 8 ] , for each source node v v , it is not necessary for intermediate nodes to forward all prefixes of paths with extremity v v for eventually finding one path forming a cycle containing v v , but forwarding a constant number of prefixes suffices. Our density theorem is used to show that, if a node v v has to forward more than O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) prefixes of paths in total (i.e., corresponding to more than O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}) source nodes v v ), then there must exist a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle in the graph G G . More precisely, our theorem states the following. Theorem 2 . Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be an n n -node graph. For every v ∈ V v\in V , and every integer ℓ ≥ 1 \ell\geq 1 , let R ℓ ​ ( v ) ⊆ V R_{\ell}(v)\subseteq V be the set of nodes that are reachable from v v by a simple path of length exactly ℓ \ell . For every integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , if there exist v ∈ V v\in V and ℓ ∈ { 1 , … , k − 1 } \ell\in\{1,\dots,k-1\} such that ∑ u ∈ R ℓ ​ ( v ) deg ⁡ ( u ) > 6 ⋅ ( ( 2 ​ k ) ℓ + ℓ ​ k ) ⋅ n , \sum_{u\in R_{\ell}(v)}\deg(u)>6\cdot((2k)^{\ell}+\ell k)\cdot n, then there is a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle in G G . Combining the Representative Lemma by Monien [ 9 ] with our density theorem, our algorithm for heavy 2 ​ k 2k -cycles boils down to flooding the network for k k steps with path-prefixes originated at all heavy nodes v v , under the following simple condition: At every step ℓ ∈ { 1 , … , k } \ell\in\{1,\dots,k\} , if a node u u has to forward more than 4 ⋅ ( 2 ​ k ) ℓ ⋅ n 1 − 1 / k 4\cdot(2k)^{\ell}\cdot n^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}} path-prefixes then u u rejects. If flooding is completed without any rejection then the Representative Lemma guarantees that a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle will be detected if any. Instead, if flooding aborts at some rejecting nodes, then the density theorem guarantees than this rejection is legitimate as there must exist a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle. 2 Model and Preliminary Results This section recalls the standard (Broadcast) CONGEST model, and takes benefit of the 4-cycle detection algorithm in [ 4 ] for introducing the reader with some of the techniques that will be reused throughout the paper. This is particularly the case of the Representative Lemma by Monien [ 9 ] , which is the basis for implementing a filtering technique enabling to bound the congestion of cycle-detection algorithms. We shall illustrate this technique on the detection of 2 ​ k 2k -cycles containing only nodes of low degree (how low depends on the value of k k ). 2.1 The Broadcast CONGEST Model The CONGEST model [ 10 ] assumes n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 processing nodes connected by a network modeled as an arbitrary n n -node graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) . (All graphs are supposed to be connected and simple, i.e., no multiple edges nor self-loops.) Each node v v in G G has an identifier 𝗂𝖽 ⁡ ( v ) \mathsf{id}(v) taken from a polynomial range of identifiers, and thus encoded on O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) bits. Each identifier is supposed to be unique in the network. Computation proceeds as a sequence of synchronous rounds. All nodes start synchronously, at round 1. At each round, every node v v can (1) send an O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) -bit message to each of its neighbors in G G , i.e., to each node u ∈ N ⁡ ( v ) u\in N(v) , (2) receive the messages sent by its neighbors, and (3) perform some individual computation. No limit is placed on the amount of computation that each node performs at each round. Initially, every node v v knows its identifier 𝗂𝖽 ⁡ ( v ) \mathsf{id}(v) and the size n n of the graph it belongs to. No other information about the graph are provided to the nodes. All nodes perform the same algorithm, but the behavior of the nodes may vary along the course of execution of that algorithm, depending on the information acquired by them in each round (including their IDs). The Broadcast CONGEST model is a restricted variant of the CONGEST model which requires each node to send the same O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) bit message to all its neighbors, at every round. (Of course, the messages sent by a same node v v at two different rounds may be different.) 2.2 Deciding C 4 C_{4} -Freeness The optimal (deterministic) algorithm for detecting 4-cycles in [ 4 ] can be artificially rewritten as Algorithm 1 . In both phases, paths are broadcast among the nodes in the networks. That is, every node v v participating in the broadcast sends its identifiers to its neighbors, which concatenate their identifiers, and forward the resulting 1-edge path to their neighbors. Indeed, let us assume that one is questioning whether there is a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle in the n n -node graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) containing a given node v ∈ V v\in V . One way to proceed is to let v v broadcast its identifier for k k rounds. That is, at round 1, v v sends 𝗂𝖽 ⁡ ( v ) \mathsf{id}(v) to all its neighbors. We start here by describing our algorithm for deciding C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness, and then we proceed to the proof of Theorem 1 . 3.1 Algorithm Description Algorithm 2 provides a synthetic description of our algorithm for the detection of 2 ​ k 2k -cycles. As Algorithm 1 , it is split into two phases, one aiming at detecting light 2 ​ k 2k -cycles, and one aiming at detecting heavy 2 ​ k 2k -cycles, where a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle is light if it contains only nodes of degree smaller than n 1 / k n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}} , and it is heavy otherwise. This result is based on a new result in graph theory, which essentially states that when some form of “local density” exceeds a certain threshold (that depends on k k ) in a graph, that graph must contain a 2 ​ k 2k -cycle. We point out that our deterministic algorithm for C 2 ​ k C_{2k} -freeness can be used to solve the same problem in the Quantum CONGEST model in O ~ ​ ( n 1 / 2 − 1 / 2 ​ k ) \tilde{O}(n^{1/2-1/2k}) rounds, following the same approach as in [ 6 ] . Informally, the approach consists in three steps : (1) apply the “diameter reduction” technique of [ 5 ] , which allows to reduce the problem to graphs of polylogarithmic diameter, (2) operate a “congestion reduction” on Algorithm 2 to make it work in a constant number of CONGEST rounds, at the cost of reducing the probability for cycle detection to Θ ⁡ ( n − 1 + 1 / k ) \Theta(n^{{-1+1/k}}) , and (3) eventually use an “amplification technique” based on quantum Grover search to obtain a constant probability of detecting a C 2 ​ k C_{2k} , if exists, in O ~ ​ ( n 1 / 2 − 1 / 2 ​ k ) \tilde{O}(n^{1/2-1/2k}) rounds. The same round complexity was already attained in [ 6 ] , but the new algorithm is considerably simpler. Note that the constant 6 ⋅ ( ( 2 ​ k ) ℓ + ℓ ​ k ) 6\cdot((2k)^{\ell}+\ell k) in our density theorem is not tight, at least for some values of k k and ℓ \ell . Arguably one of the main open problems in the field of distributed subgraph detection under the CONGEST model is whether O ⁡ ( n 1 − 1 / k ) O(n^{1-1/k}) is the best round-complexity that can be achieved for 2 ​ k 2k -cycle detection, whether it be by deterministic or randomized algorithms, up to polylogarithmic factors.
