                                                                        THE KLS CONSTANT IS O(log1/4 n)

                                                                                      BRAYDEN LETWIN


                                                  Abstract. We confirm the Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits conjecture for quadratic forms: if X ∼ µ
                                                  is an isotropic log-concave random vector in Rn then for any symmetric matrix M one has that
                                                                           VarX∼µ (⟨M X, X⟩) ⩽ 2 · EX∼µ |∇ ⟨M X, X⟩|2 .
                                                  As an application, we apply the above to M = EX∼µ (⟨X, θ⟩ X ⊗ X) for θ ∈ S n−1 and show that
                                                  the Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits constant ψn satisfies that
                                                                                        ψn ⩽ C · log1/4 n
                                                  for some absolute constant C > 0.

                                                                                      1. Introduction
                                              The Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits (KLS) conjecture, introduced by Kannan, Lovász, and Simonovits
                                           [36], is one of the central open problems in asymptotic convex geometry. In one of its equivalent (up
                                           to absolute constants) formulations, it asserts that every isotropic log-concave probability measure
                                           µ on Rn satisfies a Poincaré inequality
                                                                            VarX∼µ (f (X)) ⩽ C · EX∼µ |∇f (X)|2 ,
                                           for every locally Lipschitz function f : Rn → R, where C > 0 is an absolute constant. Here
                                           X ∼ µ denotes a random vector X with law equal to µ. For general background in the theory of
                                           asymptotic convex geometry we refer to [3, 4, 15], and for treatise specifically centered around the
                                           KLS conjecture, we refer to [1, 46, 54].
                                              Besides its intrinsic interest, the conjecture is a cornerstone of high-dimensional probability, with
                                           far-reaching applications and connections to fundamental problems in high-dimensional geometry,
                                           information theory, statistics, and theoretical computer science. The conjecture grew out of the
                                           "localization method", which has grown to be modernly fruitful in computer science and its ap-
                                           plication to random walks on convex bodies [36, 37, 57]; its algorithmic role, and role in sampling
                                           log-concave distributions is developed further in [19, 20]. A transport approach to understanding
                                           KLS, based on the Föllmer process, was also developed in [59].
                                              Recall that a probability density ρ : Rn → [0, ∞) is log-concave if its support {x ∈ Rn : ρ(x) > 0}
                                           is a convex set and log ρ is concave on the support of ρ. Given a probability measure µ on Rn , we
                                           say that µ is log-concave if, for any compact subsets A and B of Rn and 0 ⩽ λ ⩽ 1 one has that
                                                                            µ(λA + (1 − λ)B) ⩾ µ(A)λ µ(B)1−λ .
                                           By a theorem of Borell [12], a probability measure µ on Rn is log-concave if and only if, upon writing
                                           H = aff(supp µ), it has a log-concave density ρ : H → [0, ∞) with respect to Lebesgue measure
                                           on H. The uniform measure on a convex body, as well as a standard Gaussian measure are both
                                           canonical examples of a log-concave measure. If X ∼ µ is a random vector in Rn , we say that X is
                                           log-concave if its law is log-concave. Furthermore, we say that X (or µ if X ∼ µ) is isotropic if it
                                           has zero mean and identity covariance:
                                                                          EX∼µ (X) = 0,     and     CovX∼µ (X) = Id.

   As discussed above, the KLS conjecture asserts that every isotropic log-concave probability mea-
sure µ on Rn satisfies CP (µ) ⩽ C, where C > 0 is an absolute constant independent of n. Equiv-
alently, supn⩾2 ψn ⩽ C. The KLS conjecture is known for ℓnp -balls and for broad subclasses of
generalized Orlicz balls; see Kolesnikov and Milman [49, 51].
   The conjecture is closely connected with thin-shell concentration [9,32,33,47,61], Gaussian behav-
ior of linear marginals of convex bodies [2, 11, 25, 38, 39, 56], and was known to imply the now solved
slicing problem of Bourgain: [6, 8, 13, 26, 45]. Links between KLS and a generalized central-limit
conjecture were developed up to polylogarithmic factors in n in [35]. Further quantitative central
limit theorems for sums of high-dimensional log-concave random vectors appear in [27, 28].
   With a series of breakthroughs initiated by Eldan’s method of stochastic-localization [24], Lee
and Vempala
