                                                     For a graph G, let c1 (G) be the largest distortion necessary to embed any shortest-path metric on G into ℓ1 , and
                                                 for any natural number n, m ∈ N, denote Kn,m as the complete bipartite graph. In this note, we caculate the value of
                                                                                                  3k−2
                                                 c1 (K2,n ), more precisely we prove c1 (K2,n ) = 2k−1 where k = ⌈ n2 ⌉.

3       Upper Bounds
By the monotonicity, we only have to prove c1 (K2,2k ) ≤ 3k−2
                                                           2k−1 for all k ∈ N. Let w : E(K2,2k ) → R≥0 be an arbitrary
weight of K2,2k . We first note the following compactness argument. See Deza and Laurent[2] for the proof.
Proposition 4. For any compact metric space (X, d) and metric space Y , if X can embed into Y with distortion D + ε
for all ε > 0, then X can embed into Y with distortion D.
    Therefore, by scaling and approximating w with rational valued weight, we only have to deal with the graph G, which
is obtained from subdividing K2,2k several times. We point out that cutting loose paths does not affect the distortion.
We use the statement, as seen in Chakrabarti et al. [1], in the following manner.
Proposition 5. Let G be any bipartite graph and dG be its shortest path metric. For any x, y ∈ V (G) and the path P
between x, y with its length is strictly larger than dG (x, y) and all the inner points of P are degree 2.
    Then, there is a non-trival cut R of inner points of P , which has following properties: let H be the graph obtained
from G by shrinking the edges between R and V (G)\R, then for any embedding f : V (H) → ℓ1 , there exists an embedding
g : V (G) → ℓ1 which has the same distortion to f .
   By applying proposition 5, we only have to consider when all the paths of G between two endpoints of G are equal.
