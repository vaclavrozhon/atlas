## Formal target and definitions

The quantifier order `forall R, exists C,k, forall T,U of R's PL type, exists a path` is the fixed-manifold claim. Constants are positive integers; restricting the degree to at least one loses no polynomial upper bounds since endpoint tetrahedron counts are positive. A monomial in their sum dominates any fixed bivariate polynomial. The complete negation correctly puts one reference type before all constants and exponents, lets the endpoint pair vary with those constants, and quantifies over every legal path. No efficient way to find constants or a path is inadvertently required.

Finite pure abstract 3-complexes with every vertex link PL-homeomorphic to the tetrahedron boundary or a filled triangle give the standard sphere/ball local conditions. In this dimension these conditions, with connectivity, describe compact connected combinatorial PL 3-manifolds including their boundary. The definition of PL equivalence by simplicially isomorphic finite linear subdivisions is appropriate. The boundary generated by triangles incident to one tetrahedron is correct. No orientability restriction or boundary marking is introduced. A listed maximal tetrahedron determines its entire face closure, so the proposed representation specifies the objects without adding geometric data.

The link explicitly requires disjointness. The bistellar data `A != empty`, `B != empty`, `|A|+|B|=5`, `B notin K`, `lk_K(A)=boundary Delta B` correctly give the four standard replacements. Removing exactly the faces containing A and inserting `boundary Delta A * Delta B` retains all common boundary faces. The missing-face condition prevents a duplicate replacement simplex. For `(card A, card B)=(4,1)` the fresh B vertex gives the 1-to-4 move, including a tetrahedron incident to the manifold boundary. Thus the draft avoids an incorrect demand that the whole closed replacement ball lie strictly inside the manifold interior. Requiring the resulting complex to remain admissible is safe.

The boundary attachment is also correct: the **entire** intersection of the new closed tetrahedron with K must be exactly a union of one, two or three closed facets and their subfaces, inside the boundary subcomplex. This excludes additional contacts at vertices or edges and excludes gluing along the whole sphere or no facet. The inverse is unambiguous because it is the reverse relation of an allowed attachment; equivalently the retained complex is generated by the remaining tetrahedra, with unsupported faces discarded. Requiring both endpoints to be admissible excludes deleting the final tetrahedron or introducing lower-dimensional residues. These are the conventional elementary shellings/inverse shellings supported by the checked primary definitions.

Free simplicial relabeling, a zero-length path for isomorphic endpoints, and unrestricted intermediate tetrahedron counts are all specified. The full Lean acceptance criterion is an exact existence/refutation claim, so the numerical 1/100 tolerance does not apply. Five English summary sentences are present. No proof strategy is supplied.
