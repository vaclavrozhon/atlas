# Importance order in all 35 categories

Importance ordering is an editorial assessment of foundational significance, breadth of consequences, and influence on other questions. Each assessed card gives a reason based on its actual mathematical target. Nearby scores express comparable editorial priorities, not precise scientific measurements. Unassessed drafts share a provisional score of 50; their relative order is only an ID tie-break, not an importance judgment. The first 5/2 places in each large/small category are an explicit editorial focus selection led by scientific importance, with topical diversity distinguishing comparably important candidates. Focus ordering does not alter importance scores; remaining candidates use score and ID order. The primary target is 500 problems; Top 100 is its priority subset with secondary editorial attention. Top 100, Top 500 and the legacy Top 1000 view use the first 5/2, 25/10 and 50/20 category places respectively, all from the same ranking. Category positions include all candidates and remain fixed when filtering. In the live reader, shared vote scores take precedence and this editorial order only breaks vote ties. Publication date, source date, and card detail do not increase importance. Inactive cards, including resolved records, are archived outside the ranking without changing their historical importance score. Importance review does not establish that a saved question is still open.

958 records have an editorial importance assessment; 0 remain provisional.

The full ordered candidate list, with reasons and statuses, is in [importance-ranking.csv](importance-ranking.csv). Below are the first ten candidates in each category. The focus prefix prioritizes importance and uses diversity among comparable candidates; full decisions are in [benchmark-selection.md](benchmark-selection.md).
