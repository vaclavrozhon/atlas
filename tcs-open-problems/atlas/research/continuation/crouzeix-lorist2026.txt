                                                        A SOLUTION TO CROUZEIX’S CONJECTURE


                                                             EMIEL LORIST        AND FELIX L. SCHWENNINGER


                                                    Abstract. We provide a proof of Crouzeix’s conjecture, which combines the
                                                    tools developed previously for weaker estimates with a simple perturbation
                                                    lemma for 2-dilations. Applying the lemma to the iterates f n in the double-
                                                    layer potential representation yields the conjectured bound.

                                             In 2004, Crouzeix [6] conjectured that for any dimension d ∈ N, any linear
                                           operator A on H = Cd and any polynomial p with complex coefficients,
                                           (1)                             ∥p(A)∥ ≤ 2 max |p(z)|,
                                                                                        z∈W (A)

                                           where W (A) = {⟨Ax, x⟩ : x ∈ H, ∥x∥ = 1} denotes the numerical range of A and ∥·∥
                                           denotes the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm. Subsequently Crouzeix
                                           [7] showed that (1) holds if 2 is replaced by 11.08. Ten years later,
                                                                                                             √ Crouzeix and
                                           Palencia [12] showed that √    the constant can be lowered to 1 +  2. Later, it was
                                           shown in [19] that 1 + 2 is never attained. The conjecture has been shown for
                                           many special classes of matrices and operators in recent years [1, 11, 15, 22]. Let
                                           us mention that the case d = 2 was already obtained in [6] and the case where the
                                           numerical range is a disk goes back to a classical result by Okubo and Ando on
                                           2-contractions [21].
                                              The main technical tool used to derive results for general matrices (and opera-
                                           tors) in most references above is the operator-valued double-layer potential, which
                                           originates in the seminal work of Delyon and Delyon [13]. For the main features of
                                           this technique, we refer to [27]. The use of the double-layer potential was refined
                                           in [12], which also initiated a research program to abstract the function-theoretic
                                           “hard analysis” into operator-theoretic “soft analysis” [2, 5, 25, 26]. This note
                                           continues and completes this abstract approach by supplying the final piece of the
                                           puzzle.
                                              We note in passing the efforts to use more “hard analysis” insights to resolve the
                                           conjecture, including the use of Blaschke products B, which after composition with
                                           a conformal mapping, realize the (unknown) optimal constant in (1), see [2, 3, 5].
                                           In particular, we mention the more recent proof of the d = 2 case in [20].
                                              During the preparation of this manuscript, a proof of Crouzeix’s conjecture ap-
                                           peared independently in [17] using an approach involving function-theoretic repre-
                                           sentations of matrix-valued functions. Our proof instead uses the operator-theoretic
                                           Lemma 1.
                                              In the rest of this note, all Hilbert spaces H are complex, inner products are
                                           linear in the first variable, and L(H) denotes the space of bounded linear operators
                                           on H.

contraction. Indeed, if the perturbations are uniformly bounded over the powers of
the operator and a commutativity condition holds, then the operator norm cannot
exceed 2.

works. This can be shortened by an abstract argument, see Section 3.

                         3. Remarks and complements
   In this section, we collect several observations on Theorem 3 and its proof, and
place the underlying ideas and techniques in their historical context.
     (i) Comparison of the ingredients used with earlier approaches.
         The proof of Theorem 3 does not use that α is contractive (as a consequence
         of the convexity of the domain), which was the key new ingredient used
         in Crouzeix–Palencia’s improvement [12] of the old estimate as well as in
         the alternative proof in [25], see also [5, 26]. In some sense, nearly all
         facts used in the proof of Theorem 3 were already present in Crouzeix’s
         original proof [7], with the minor exception that he did not explicitly state
         that 2Φ(f ) − f (A) is given by α(f )(A)∗ . This identity is needed to show
         the commutativity assumption in Lemma 1. However, he did prove the
         estimate ∥2Φ(f ) − f (A)∥ ≤ 9.08 ∥f ∥∞ , which implies supn∈N ∥En ∥ < ∞.
         Additionally, the use of Φ(1) = I to define the isometry V is new.
    (ii) Extremal functions and vectors.
         The central object in the proof of Lemma 1, translated to the proof in
         Theorem 3, is
          m1 = ℜ⟨E1 T x, x⟩ = ℜ⟨f (A)α(f )(A)x, x⟩ = ℜ⟨(f α(f ))(A)x, x⟩,
        which has already played a central role in previous approaches. Indeed,
        for an extremal pair (f, x) ∈ A(Ω) × H, that is, unit-norm elements f
        and x such that ∥f (A)x∥ = suph∈A(Ω),∥h∥∞ ≤1 ∥h(A)∥, this expression was
        studied in [2, 26]. In [26] it was shown that |⟨(f α(f ))(A)x, √
                                                                       x⟩| ≤ 1 and thus
        m1 ≥ −1. Using Remark 2(i), this yields the constant 1 + 2. Afterwards,
        in [27] it was shown that m1 ≥ 0 implies that Crouzeix’s conjecture is true,
        which is essentially Remark 2(i). This was also used in [10] to reprove
        Choi’s result [4] for weighted shift matrices.
           Recently, it was shown in [20] that m1 ≥ 0 holds in dimension d = 2
        if Ω = W (A) (strictly speaking, one has to replace W (A) by its interior).
        However, examples of 3 × 3 matrices can be constructed such that m1
        is strictly negative. Furthermore, for d = 2 such examples can also be
        constructed if the set Ω is chosen strictly larger than W (A). Thus, the fact
        that Lemma 1 allows m1 < 0 is not just an artifact of our proof.
           We also remark that our proof does not exploit any specific properties
        of the extremal functions or vectors [2, 3], or the related extremal measures
        [26].
    (iii) The refinement from n = 1 to all n ≥ 1.
          The identity (2) for n = 1, combined with E1 = α(f )(A) and the contrac-√
          tivity of α, see [12, 27], also implies the weaker spectral constant 1 + 2.
          Indeed, as in [25], (2) with n = 1 gives
                     T ∗ T T ∗ T = T ∗ T (2V ∗ Q∗ V ) T − T ∗ T E1 T.
        For an extremal f , with T , E1 and κ as in the proof of Theorem 3, the first
        term on the right-hand side has norm at most 2κ3 and the second term
        satisfies
         ∥T ∗ T E1 T ∥ ≤ ∥f (A)∥∥(f α(f )f )(A)∥ ≤ κ∥θ∥∥f ∥2∞ ∥α(f )∥∞ ≤ κ2 .

        This yields κ4 ≤ 2κ3 + κ2 , and√hence κ ≤ 1 + 2.
           The improvement from 1 + 2 to 2 in our argument comes from leverag-
        ing the identities (2) simultaneously for all n ≥ 1, and hence from combining
        the algebraic structure of the functional calculus with the norm estimates
        in a more refined way. A related phenomenon occurs in Esterle’s proof of

        the zero-two law for cosine functions [14, Section 3]: there, an algebraic
        identity for cosine functions is combined with a refined norm inequality to
        upgrade a non-sharp estimate to the sharp bound. This argument may be
        viewed as a loose inspiration for the proof of Lemma 1.
   (iv) Extensions and limitations
        The proof of Theorem 3 can be adapted to show the following general
        abstract result, which settles a variant of [25, Question 4.1], see also [5,
        Conjecture 1.2 and Theorem 1.3]:
             Let A be a uniform algebra, α : A → A a unital, antilinear,
             bounded map and θ : A → L(H) a unital, bounded homomor-
             phism. If Φ = 12 (θ(·) + θ(α(·))∗ ) is completely positive, then
             ∥θ∥ ≤ 2.
        To see this, one uses Arveson’s extension and Stinespring’s dilation theorem,
        see [24], to extend Φ to a C ∗ -algebra C and to deduce the existence of a
        Hilbert space K, an isometry V : H → K and a ∗-homomorphism π : C →
        L(K) such that
                                  Φ(f ) = V ∗ π(f )V.
        For f ∈ A with ∥f ∥ = 1 set T = θ(f ) and Q = π(f ), which settles the claim
        as in the proof of Theorem 3. Note that the Φ in the proof of Theorem
        3 indeed extends to a positive, and thus completely positive, map on the
        commutative C ∗ -algebra C(∂Ω) of the continuous functions on ∂Ω.
           We point out that neither our proof nor Jin’s proof [17] can be used in a
        direct way to derive the completely bounded case of Crouzeix’s conjecture.
        For this version of the conjecture and related recent results, see [5, 16]. In
        our argument, the obstruction is reflected by the commutativity assumption
        in Lemma 1, which is not available after matrix amplification.
   (v) Extensions to other spectral sets
       The abstract result from (iv) above immediately implies the optimal spec-
       tral constant 2 for the quantum annulus, see [8, 9, 17, 18, 23, 28]. The
       generalization of Lemma 1 described in Remark 2(ii) can be used to de-
       rive spectral constants for other (possibly non-convex) sets other than the
       numerical range, see [9].
