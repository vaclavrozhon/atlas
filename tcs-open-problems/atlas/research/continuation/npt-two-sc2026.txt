                                                                                                 I.   INTRODUCTION

                                               Pure entanglement plays a key role in various quantum-information tasks outperforming the classical counterparts
                                            such as teleportation [1] and error correction [2]. Pure entanglement inevitably evolves into a mixed state due to
                                            the noise in a lab. Hence, the task of distilling pure entanglement from mixed states is a fundamental task in
arXiv:2607.23416v1 [quant-ph] 26 Jul 2026




                                            quantum information theory. The states are called distillable when the task is feasible. That is, a nonzero amount of
                                            pure entanglement can be asymptotically extracted from many copies of mixed states through local operations and
                                            classical communication (LOCC) [3–5]. Distillable entanglement is related to many issues of quantum information
                                            and physics, such as distinguishability of Bell states [6], entanglement negativity [7], thermodynamically quantifying
                                            quantum correlations [8], quantum communication through an unmodulated spin chain [9], entangling power of passive
                                            optical elements [10], squashed entanglement [11], the generation of security keys [12], entanglement in noninertial
                                            frames [13], capacity of quantum channels [14], extremality of Gaussian states [15], random states in high-dimensional
                                            bipartite systems [16], discord in measurements [17], lattice gauge theories [18], resource theory of coherence [19]
                                            and so on. Recent experiments indicate that distillation protocols with limited resources are operationally relevant,
                                            especially in photonic and high-dimensional platforms where multiple copies and collective operations are costly [20].
                                            In particular, two-qutrit Werner states have been prepared experimentally, and stochastic local filters have been used
                                            to reveal hidden useful quantum features [21], providing further motivation for the finite-copy distillability problem.
                                               It has been shown that distillable states have non-positive-partial transpose (NPT) [22, 23]. In contrast, PPT bound
                                            entangled states have been constructed in [24, 25]. The long-standing distillability problem asks whether all NPT
                                            entangled states are distillable and become available resources. The problem has attracted considerable attentions,
                                            for a review see [26]. The distillability problem has been investigated using semidefinite programming [27]. However
                                            for any integer n, there are n-copy undistillable but (n + 1)-copy distillable entangled states [28]. So the distillability
                                            problem seriously undermines the idea of computer verification. Recently, the problem has been seen as one of the
                                            five fundamental problems in the scope of theoretical quantum information [29]. It is widely believed that the answer
                                            to the problem might be negative, and the existence of NPT bound entangled states would imply the non-additivity
                                            of distillable entanglement and activation of bound entanglement [30, 31]. It also would manifest the irreversibility in
                                            asymptotic manipulations of entanglement [32].
                                               Technically, qubit-qudit NPT states are proven to be distillable [33]. The distillable entanglement can also be
                                            verified via the violation of reduction criterion and creation of entanglement witness [34, 35]. From a mathematical
                                            point of view, the rank of a target NPT state heavily determines its distillability [36], and the bipartite NPT states
                                            with rank at most four turn out to be 1-distillable [37, 38]. Further, the distillbility problem has turned out to be
                                            closely related to the well-known family of Werner states on Cd ⊗ Cd

  Activation shows that one-copy undistillability may be lost after an auxiliary entangled state is supplied. More
precisely, every bipartite NPT state ρ is either one-copy distillable or admits a PPT bound entangled state σ such
that ρ ⊗ σ is one-copy distillable [35]. In particular, via a reduction to Werner states, such activator states can be
chosen to be universal and arbitrarily close to the separable set for each fixed local dimension [49]. Consequently, if
NPT bound entangled states exist, this phenomenon would imply that distillable entanglement is superadditive and
nonconvex [30]. Our next corollary provides a contrasting result, showing that two independent NPT Werner states
cannot activate each other’s one-copy distillability.
