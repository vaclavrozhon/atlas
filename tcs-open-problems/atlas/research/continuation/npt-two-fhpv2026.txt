                                                     Abstract. Problem 5 in Five Open Problems in Quantum Information Theory [PRX Quantum 3, 010101
                                                     (2022)], asks whether the two-ququart Werner state ϱ(4, − 12 ) is two-copy distillable, where ϱ(d, α) =
                                                     (I+αF )/(d2 +αd). We answer it in the negative. To this end, we show the following stronger statement:
                                                     for all C ∈ Md1 d2 (C) of rank at most r ≤ d1 d2 , tr1 (C)∥2F + ∥tr2 (C)∥2F ≤ r∥C∥2F + r1 |tr(C)|2 . A
                                                     result by Costa Rico on the equivalence of this inequality with two-copy undistillability at r = 2 then
                                                     settles Problem 5: ϱ(4, − 12 ) is not two-copy distillable. Furthermore, we show that ϱ(d, α) is two-copy
                                                     undistillable for every d ≥ 2, if and only if α ≥ − 21 . Thus, the one and two-copy distillability regions
                                                     of ϱ(d, α) coincide. These results have been found and written up with AI tools, pointing towards a
                                                     structural change affecting the field of quantum information and computation.

                                            rank at most two across A1 A2 : B1 B2 satisfies ⟨ψ|(ϱTB )⊗2 |ψ⟩ < 0, where TB is partial transposition

                                            non-positivity of the partial transpose (NPT)—the Peres criterion [3]—is necessary for distillability [2].

1.3. The open problem. The concrete question addressed here is the following, listed as Problem 5
in Horodecki, Rudnicki and Życzkowski’s Five Open Problems in Quantum Information Theory [9].
Problem 1.1 (Problem 5 of [9]). Decide whether the Werner state ϱ(4, − 12 ) of two ququarts, d = 4,
defined in Eq. (2), is two-copy distillable.
    The answer given in this work is negative [shown in Cor. B]. More generally, we ask:
Problem 1.2. For which d and α is ϱ(d, α) two-copy distillable?
  We show that for every d ≥ 2, the Werner state ϱ(d, α) is two-copy undistillable if and only if
α ≥ − 12 [shown in Cor. C]. Thus every Werner state is two-copy undistillable if and only if it is
one-copy undistillable.

  The proof presented in this work was found with ChatGPT Sol-5.6 and refined with Claude Opus and
Fable. All results have been carefully checked by the authors.

   During preparation of this manuscript we became aware of a similar work by Fu et al, arXiv:2607.21367
and a manuscript in preparation by Kishor Bharti, Rishikesh Gajjala, and Tobias Haug. They seem to use
a different proof strategy but also use a variant of Lemma 6.6.

                          2. Undistillability from a partial trace inequality
    To answer Problems 1.1 and 1.2, we will make use of the following partial trace inequality.
Theorem 2.1 (Partial-trace criterion; Costa Rico [10, Theorem 1], in the norm form of Costa Rico
and Wolf [11, Eq. (31)]). Let d ≥ 2 and α ∈ [−1, 0). The Werner state ϱ(d, α) is two-copy undistillable
if and only if every C ∈ Md2 (C) with rank(C) ≤ 2 satisfies
                                                               1
                           ∥ tr1 (C)∥2F + ∥ tr2 (C)∥2F ≤          ∥C∥2F + |α| | tr(C)|2 .                (3)
                                                              |α|
   Costa Rico [10] states the criterion for n copies and in the equivalent quadratic form qα (C) ≥ 0,
where qα (C) = ∥C∥2F + α(∥ tr1 (C)∥2F + ∥ tr2 (C)∥2F ) + α2 | tr(C)|2 ; only n = 2 is used here. Writing
α = −t with t > 0 and dividing q−t (C) ≥ 0 by t gives the norm form (3) stated above, which is the
form recorded by Costa Rico and Wolf [11, Eq. (31)] and the one applied here.
   Costa Rico and Wolf [11] prove that (3) holds for every rank-two C when α ≥ − 13 , and thus
Werner states are two-copy undistillable in this range. Our aim is to show that this range extends to
α = − 12 .

Corollary B. The two-ququart Werner state ϱ(4, − 21 ) is not two-copy distillable.

                                             9. Back to distillability
  We can now answer the first question.
Problem 1.1 (Problem 5 of [9]). Decide whether the Werner state ϱ(4, − 12 ) of two ququarts, d = 4,
defined in Eq. (2), is two-copy distillable.
  The answer is:
Corollary B. The two-ququart Werner state ϱ(4, − 12 ) is not two-copy distillable.
Proof. Theorem A with d1 = d2 = 4 and r = 2 states that every C ∈ M16 (C) of rank at most two
satisfies ∥ tr1 (C)∥2F + ∥ tr2 (C)∥2F ≤ 2∥C∥2F + 21 | tr(C)|2 . This is condition (3) of Theorem 2.1 at
d = 4, α = − 12 . It follows that ϱ(4, − 21 ) is two-copy undistillable. This ends the proof.        □
  We can also answer the second main question:
