                                               Abstract
                                         We prove that satisfiability for existential Presburger arithmetic with divisibility (EPAD) is NEXP-
                                         hard. Together with the known NEXP upper bound, this establishes NEXP-completeness. The
                                         lower bound is obtained by encoding succinct Boolean formulas whose satisfying assignments may
                                         have exponential length. A central difficulty is to represent, within a polynomial-size EPAD formula,
                                         the exponentially long integers arising in this encoding.
arXiv:2606.14167v3 [cs.LO] 19 Jul 2026




                                             To address this difficulty, we introduce fixed-width arithmetic logic circuits (ALCs), whose
                                         gates perform addition, multiplication, and bit shifts. We show that polynomial-size uniform
                                         ALC families compute exactly the functions in FPSPACE, while their succinct exponential-size
                                         counterpart computes exactly the functions in FEXP. In both cases, the computed functions
                                         admit polynomial-size functional definitions in EPAD. This provides the compressed arithmetic
                                         needed for the lower-bound reduction.
                                             The same construction yields NEXP-hardness for nonerasing word equations with Presburger
                                         length constraints over a fixed two-letter alphabet. We also study the elimination of divisibility
                                         constraints by enumerating their possible quotients. Earlier work identified an NP fragment in
                                         which the variables can be ordered so that divisibility dependencies always move forward through
                                         the order. We introduce a different fragment, called merge-absorptive, in which bounded-quotient
                                         divisibilities can successively connect all variable components. This fragment is polynomial-time
                                         recognizable and admits complete finite-quotient elimination, yet its satisfiability problem remains
                                         NEXP-complete. Finally, we show that the elimination process may necessarily produce equations
                                         with exponentially large coefficients.

 1     Introduction
Existential Presburger arithmetic is NP-complete [9]. Existential Presburger arithmetic with
divisibility (EPAD) additionally allows atoms L | M , where L and M are affine terms. Such
an atom holds when M = qL for some integer q. Decidability was proved independently
by Lipshitz [16] and Beltyukov [2]. Lechner, Ouaknine, and Worrell proved an NEXP
upper bound [14]. Previously, only NP-hardness was known, and several works discussed
the possibility of an NP upper bound [3, 8].
    Divisibility constraints can enforce large fixed ratios without writing those ratios as
                                                                            t
coefficients. For example, polynomially many constraints can force r = (22 − 1)x. We use
this fact both in the hardness reduction and in the coefficient-growth example for finite-
quotient elimination. The upper bound in the following theorem is known; our contribution
is the matching lower bound.

▶ Theorem 1 (Main theorem). EPAD satisfiability is NEXP-complete under polynomial-time
many-one reductions.

Overview of the lower bound. We introduce fixed-width arithmetic logic circuits (ALCs)
with modular addition, modular multiplication, and logical shifts. We write ALC for the
functions computed by polynomial-time uniform polynomial-size ALC families and prove
ALC = FPSPACE. For the inclusion FPSPACE ⊆ ALC, a polynomial-space predicate
for an indexed output bit is represented by a QBF, and packed truth tables evaluate all
assignments simultaneously.
    The function computed by each ALC gate is definable in EPAD. Polynomial-size homoge-
neous divisibility systems define multiplication by powers of two whose exponents are given
in binary. A separate two-atom construction defines a bounded product. These formulas
yield ALC ⊆ FEPAD, and hence FPSPACE ⊆ FEPAD.
    For a uniformly succinct Exact-1-in-3 formula, each clause condition is a linear equation
in variables ranging over {−1, +1}. Powers of four combine all clause equations into one
linear congruence, while an integer parameter specifies the values of the first assignment
variables. A Chinese-remainder construction represents the {−1, +1}-valued variables by
one bounded integer, and two divisibility atoms involving differences of squares impose the
congruence and the representation. A local conversion from the succinct Cook–Levin 3CNF
to Exact-1-in-3 completes the reduction. The proof of Theorem 1 is given in Section 6.

Finite-quotient elimination. Suppose that the constraints on a branch imply that a quotient
in L | M belongs to a finite set Q. The atom may then be replaced by one branch for
each equation M = cL, with c ∈ Q. We define a polynomial-time recognizable fragment,
called merge-absorptive, in which bounded-quotient atoms can successively merge all variable
components. This guarantees a sequence of replacements that eliminates every divisibility
atom. Every EPAD formula has an equisatisfiable translation to this fragment, which proves
NEXP-hardness. Since the fragment is contained in EPAD, the known upper bound applies,
and its satisfiability problem is NEXP-complete.
    Défossez, Haase, Mansutti, and Pérez identified an NP fragment based on a different
restriction [5]. Its variables admit an order such that, among all nontrivial divisibilities
entailed by the system, the greatest variable with respect to that order occurs only on the
right-hand side. This prevents divisibility dependencies from feeding back toward earlier
variables. The two conditions are incomparable: the ordered condition controls the direction
of propagation, whereas merge-absorptivity makes quotient relations contractible.

       We also give a polynomial-size family for which a specified replacement sequence produces
    primitive equations with coefficients of exponential bit length. For the same family, any
    primitive affine equation over the distinguished variables defining the projected relation has
    a coefficient of exponential bit length. The latter statement concerns a single projected
    equation, not arbitrary equivalent representations.

         A separate consequence of the lower-bound construction concerns word equations.

    ▶ Corollary 2. Nonerasing satisfiability for positive Boolean combinations of word equations
    and quantifier-free Presburger length constraints over the fixed alphabet {a, b} is NEXP-hard.

    Related works. Galota and Vollmer also characterized FPSPACE through arithmetic
    computations [6], but with a different size and uniformity convention. Their characterization
    describes an exponentially long arithmetic computation implicitly, through the ordered
    computation paths of a polynomial-time nondeterministic machine. It is therefore closer
    to a succinct description of an exponential-size circuit than to an ordinary polynomial-
    size uniform circuit family. Our characterization instead uses genuinely polynomial-size,
    polynomial-time uniform ALC families: the exponential amount of information is packed
    into fixed-width words rather than into an implicitly represented collection of gates. This
    form of the characterization is needed here, since it permits a gate-by-gate translation
    into a polynomial-size EPAD formula.
        The linear congruence over {−1, +1}-valued variables is related to the arithmetic encoding
    of Manders and Adleman [18], but here it is applied to a succinct formula and also fixes
    the values of a specified initial sequence of assignment variables. Succinct representations
    were introduced by Galperin and Wigderson [7]; the NEXP-complete succinct-satisfiability
    setting used here is due to Papadimitriou and Yannakakis [20]. Reductions from EPAD to
    word equations with length constraints also appear in [15, 4].

▶ Definition 3 (EPAD formula satisfiability). An EPAD formula is an existential first-
order formula over integer variables using Boolean combinations of affine-linear equalities,
affine-linear inequalities, and divisibility atoms L | M between affine-linear terms. EPAD
satisfiability asks whether a given such formula is satisfiable.
   Divisibility has its ordinary integer meaning: L | M if and only if M = qL for some
q ∈ Z. Thus 0 | 0 is true and 0 | M is false for M ̸= 0. When we invoke a result stated
under the convention that the divisor must be nonzero, we separate the case L = M = 0
by a Presburger disjunction, without further comment.
▶ Example 4 (A small EPAD system). Take

   0 < x < y < r,

together with the four atoms for the stage h = 0,

   r + y | 4r,       y | r,        r − y | 2r,    r − 2y | r,

and the five atoms for the stage h = 1,

   y + x | r + 3y,        x | y,        y − x | r + y,      y − 2x | r,   y − 3x | r − y.

This is the t = 2 instance of the systems in Lemma 19. The constraints force y = 5x and
r = 15x. Adding v = r + x gives v = 16x. Divisibility can therefore describe large fixed
multiples without writing them as coefficients.                                        ◁
▶ Definition 5 (Functional EPAD). An n-input functional EPAD formula is an EPAD formula

   Φn (x, y) ≡ ∃z Θn (x, y, z),

where x ∈ {0, 1}n are Boolean free inputs and y ∈ N is the distinguished output. It computes
fn : {0, 1}n → N if, for every a ∈ {0, 1}n , there is a unique y ∈ N satisfying Φn (a, y), namely
fn (a). The class FEPAD consists of functions computed by polynomial-time uniform
polynomial-size families of such formulas. Zero-input families are allowed.
▶ Definition 6 (Arithmetic logic circuits). An arithmetic logic circuit (ALC) of word-width
W is an acyclic circuit with a distinguished output gate whose values lie in {0, . . . , 2W − 1}.
It has Boolean inputs, constants 0, 1, and gates

   u +W v = (u + v) mod 2W ,               u ×W v = uv mod 2W ,

   u ≪W s = 2s u mod 2W ,               u ≫ s = ⌊u/2s ⌋ .
Shift offsets are nonnegative binary parameters in the circuit description; shifts by at least W
return zero. A polynomial-size family is polynomial-time uniform if, from 1n , a polynomial-
time algorithm outputs the circuit, its word-width W (n) in binary, where 1 ≤ W (n) ≤ 2poly(n) ,
and its output gate. The output word is a nonnegative binary integer. The resulting function
class is ALC.
▶ Definition 7 (Function classes). The class FPSPACE consists of functions from Boolean
strings to nonnegative integers computed by deterministic polynomial-space transducers with
a one-way write-only output tape. The class FEXP is defined in the same way using
deterministic 2poly(n) -time transducers. Outputs are read in binary, and leading zeros are
immaterial. The class NEXP consists of languages accepted by nondeterministic machines
in time 2poly(n) .

    FPSPACE if and only if there are a polynomial p and a PSPACE predicate B such that
                        p(n)
         fn (x) < 22

    and, for every binary index 0 ≤ i < 2p(n) , B(x, i) = 1 exactly when the ith binary bit of fn (x)

    Proof. Suppose first that a polynomial-space machine halts after writing the output. Its state,
    input-head positions, and work tapes have only exponentially many possible configurations.
    The output tape is write-only, so a repeated configuration would force an infinite computation.
    The machine therefore writes at most exponentially many bits. To compute one indexed
    output bit, simulate the machine once to determine the output length and a second time
    to return the symbol at the requested least-significant-bit index. Both simulations use a
    polynomial-bit counter.
        Conversely, a polynomial-space transducer scans the exponentially many indices from
    the largest down to zero, evaluates B at each index, and writes the returned bit. The index
    counter has polynomial length, and the output tape is not charged to the space bound. ◀

         This characterization is standard; see, for example, [12].


     3      Polynomial-Size ALCs Compute Exactly FPSPACE

    To prove ALC ⊆ FPSPACE, we evaluate a requested output bit using polynomial space.
    For the converse inclusion, we convert a polynomial-space indexed-bit predicate to a QBF
    and represent its truth table in a large integer. Arithmetic operations on this representation
    evaluate all index assignments simultaneously.

▶ Theorem 24. For every ALC of word-width W , one can construct an EPAD formula in

     m clauses and ℓ variables. One can construct in polynomial time an Exact-1-in-3 formula

     to a satisfying assignment of F if and only if it satisfies G. The formula F has 3m clauses
     and ℓ + 4m variables. For a family of 3CNFs given by a polynomial-time clause oracle, the
     resulting Exact-1-in-3 family also has a polynomial-time clause oracle, and the variables
     inherited from the 3CNF retain their original indices.
     Proof. Write R(x, y, z) for the requirement that exactly one of the three displayed literal

 7     NEXP-Complete Finite-Quotient Normalization and Exponential
       Coefficient Growth
Finite-quotient normalization replaces a divisibility atom by equations for its possible integer
quotients. The definitions below specify the replacement step and a class of systems for
which repeated replacements remove every divisibility atom. A polynomial-size family shows
that a specified replacement sequence may produce coefficients with exponentially many bits.
▶ Definition 36 (Finite-quotient replacement). For an atom L | M on a branch, a finite-
quotient replacement with quotient set Q ⊆ Z is valid if the current branch constraints,
including the remaining divisibility atoms, together with L | M force either L = M = 0, or
L ̸= 0 and M/L ∈ Q. It creates one child for each equation M = cL, with c ∈ Q.
    The case L = M = 0 satisfies every child equation. We make a child equation primitive by
dividing the coefficients of M −cL = 0 by their greatest common divisor. A branch closes when
the accumulated constraints are inconsistent. A system reduces to divisibility-free branches if
a sequence of such replacements leaves every branch either closed or without divisibility atoms.
    The completeness constructions below require an explicit bound on the size of satisfying
assignments; the following theorem supplies it.
▶ Theorem 37 (Effective small-solution bound; Theorem 14 of [14]). There is a computable
polynomial p such that every satisfiable conjunction of affine equalities, strict affine inequali-
ties, and affine divisibility atoms of size n has an integer solution in which every variable has
bit length at most 2p(n) .

7.1    A Merge-Absorptive Fragment with Complete Finite-Quotient
       Elimination
Défossez, Haase, Mansutti, and Pérez study divisibility systems with the following property:
the variables admit an order such that, among all nontrivial divisibilities entailed by the
system, the greatest variable with respect to that order occurs only on the right-hand
side [5]. For the systems considered there, feasibility under this restriction is in NP. The
condition introduced here proceeds instead by contraction. An eligible atom has only
finitely many possible quotients and joins two previously separate variable components.
We call a system for which such atoms can successively connect all components merge-
absorptive, as formalized below.
    Recall that a positive homogeneous divisibility system consists of divisibility atoms
between homogeneous integer linear forms, together with homogeneous linear side conditions
and positivity constraints. The support of a linear form is the set of variables with nonzero
coefficient. A valuation of a component C is admissible if it is positive and satisfies all side
conditions supported on C. A form supported on C is nonvanishing on C if it is nonzero
on every admissible valuation; positivity and negativity are defined analogously.
    A homogeneous equality F = 0 is identified with the divisibility atom 0 | F . Thus
equalities may serve as linear merge atoms.
    For disjoint variable sets A, B, a form RB supported on B is side-condition bounded by A
if every variable in its support is bounded above on the real polyhedron given by the side
                                                          P
conditions on A ∪ B, positivity, and the normalization x∈A x = 1.
    Fix a partition Π of the variables, and let A, B be distinct components. Decompose
every form supported on A ∪ B as F = FA + FB . An atom L | M is a merge atom for
A, B if it mentions both components and, after possibly exchanging them, satisfies one
of the following conditions.
26   NEXP-Completeness and Exponential Coefficient Growth for EPAD


     1. Linear merge: L = 0 and M = FA + FB , where FA and FB are nonvanishing on their
        components.
     2. Bounded-divisor merge: L = PA + RB and M = QA , where PA , RB have the same strict
        sign on their components and QA is nonvanishing on A.
     3. Bounded-dividend merge: L = PA and M = TA + RB , where PA is nonvanishing on A,
        RB is nonvanishing on B, and RB is side-condition bounded by A.

     ▶ Definition 38 (Merge-absorptive systems). A positive homogeneous divisibility system is
     merge-absorptive if, starting from the singleton partition, some sequence of merge atoms
     brings all variables into one component.

     ▶ Remark 39 (Comparison with ordered divisibility systems). The ordered restriction described
     above and merge-absorptivity are incomparable. The system

        x | y,     x > 0,      y > 0,

     it only on the left. Thus one restriction organizes divisibility dependencies in a forward
     direction, while the other contracts variable components through bounded quotient relations.

      8      Summary of Results
     We proved that EPAD satisfiability is NEXP-hard; combined with the known NEXP
     upper bound, this gives NEXP-completeness. We also proved ALC = FPSPACE and
     succALC = FEXP, with both classes contained in FEPAD. The lower bound uses
     fixed-width arithmetic logic circuits, functional EPAD definitions of their gates, and a
     square-divisibility encoding of succinct Exact-1-in-3 formulas that represents the values
     of a specified initial sequence of variables by an integer parameter. We also defined the
     merge-absorptive fragment and proved NEXP-hardness for its satisfiability problem; the
     known upper bound again gives NEXP-completeness, although finite-quotient replacement
     eliminates all divisibility atoms. For a polynomial-size multiplier family, a specified replace-
     ment sequence produces primitive equations with coefficients of exponential bit length. Every
     primitive affine equation over the distinguished variables that defines the same projected
     relation also has a coefficient of exponential bit length.

▶ Theorem 56 (EPAD to nonerasing word constraints). EPAD formula satisfiability many-one

     Proof. We use the terminology of Day and Konefal [4]. A word equation is an equality α = β
     over terminal letters and word variables. A solution maps each word variable to a nonempty
     word and fixes the terminals. A length constraint is a quantifier-free Presburger formula over
     word lengths. Positive Boolean combinations use only conjunction and disjunction at the
     outer level. The length constraints themselves may be arbitrary quantifier-free Presburger
     formulas.
         We first put the EPAD input into the form used by the reduction. Replace every integer
     variable x by x+ − x− , where x+ and x− range over N. This is equisatisfiable and keeps all
     terms affine. Push negations to atoms. For each divisibility literal L | M , with L, M affine
     integer terms, introduce nonnegative variables for |L| and |M |. The absolute-value constraints
     are Presburger, and L | M over Z is equivalent to the resulting nonnegative divisibility atom.
     Then use the standard elimination of negated divisibility literals for linear arithmetic with
     divisibility [14, 13]. After adding only polynomially many existential arithmetic variables, this
     gives an equisatisfiable positive Boolean combination of quantifier-free Presburger conditions
     and unnegated divisibility atoms over nonnegative arithmetic variables. We use the divisibility
     convention fixed after Definition 3.
         We represent a nonnegative arithmetic value n by a nonempty word of length n + 1. For
     a word variable X, write val(X) = |X| − 1. Each arithmetic variable x receives a word
     variable X, and Presburger conditions are translated by replacing x with val(X). This is still
     a quantifier-free Presburger length constraint. The subtraction in val(X) is only notation; it
     can be removed by moving constants to the other side.
         A nonempty word is primitive if it is not a proper power. We use the commutation
     theorem for words: if XY = Y X, then there is a word P and integers m, n ≥ 0 such that
     X = P m and Y = P n . In particular, every word commuting with a primitive word X lies in
     X ∗ [17, Section 1.3].
         It remains to translate unnegated divisibility atoms. First consider positive lengths. For
     word variables X, Y , define
        Div+ (X, Y ) := XY = Y X ∧ aX = Zb ∧ Za = aZ,
     where Z is a fresh auxiliary word variable. For nonempty word values, Div+ (X, Y ) enforces
     exactly the relation |X| | |Y | on positive lengths. The equation Za = aZ forces Z = aj with
     j ≥ 1, and then aX = Zb forces X = aj−1 b. This word is primitive, so XY = Y X forces Y
     to be a positive power of X. Conversely, if x | y with x, y > 0, use Z = ax , X = ax−1 b, and
     Y = X y/x .
        To handle zero, define the following formula for shifted lengths:
                                                                                  
        Div0 (X, Y ) := |Y | = 1 ∨ |U | + 1 = |X| ∧ |V | + 1 = |Y | ∧ Div+ (U, V ) ,
     with fresh auxiliary word variables U, V and the auxiliary variable used inside Div+ . For
     nonempty X, Y , this formula satisfies Div0 (X, Y ) if and only if val(X) | val(Y ). If |Y | = 1,
     then val(Y ) = 0, and every nonnegative value divides 0. In the second disjunct, U and V
     have lengths val(X) and val(Y ), respectively, so Div+ gives positive divisibility. Conversely,
     if val(X) | val(Y ) and val(Y ) = 0, the first disjunct applies. If val(Y ) > 0, then val(X) > 0,
     and the positive-length construction for Div+ gives suitable U, V . Thus the shifted encoding
     has exactly the following behavior: a positive divisor uses the second disjunct and Div+ ; the
     case 0 | 0 uses the first disjunct; and 0 | m with m > 0 is rejected because neither disjunct
     can apply.
         For each unnegated divisibility atom α = L | M , introduce fresh word variables Aα , Bα ,
     add the length constraints val(Aα ) = L and val(Bα ) = M , and conjoin Div0 (Aα , Bα ). All
     auxiliary variables are interpreted existentially by the target satisfiability problem.

    The construction is equisatisfiable. From an arithmetic satisfying assignment, choose
nonempty words of the prescribed shifted lengths. The divisibility formulas are satisfied by
the previous paragraphs. Conversely, a nonerasing solution assigns each arithmetic variable x
the value |X| − 1. The translated Presburger constraints have the same truth values as their
arithmetic originals, and each Div0 formula enforces the corresponding divisibility atom. The
initial handling of signs and negated divisibility was logically equivalent, so the recovered
arithmetic assignment satisfies the original EPAD formula.
    The construction is polynomial. Each transformation is local, each affine term is encoded
by a linear-size length constraint, and each divisibility atom contributes only constantly many
word-equation atoms and length constraints, apart from the size of the terms it mentions.
