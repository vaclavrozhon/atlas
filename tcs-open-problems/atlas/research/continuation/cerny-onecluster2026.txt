                                                                                    Abstract
                                                   We prove the Černý conjecture for synchronizing one-cluster automata. More
                                               precisely, let a synchronizing automaton with state set Q, |Q| = n, have a letter a
                                               whose functional digraph has a unique cycle C of length m, and let ℓ be the least
                                               nonnegative integer for which aℓ maps Q onto C. Assume ℓ ≥ 1. For every nonempty

                                               proper subset S ⊂ C, we prove that there is a word w of length at most n such that

                                               conjecture of Kisielewicz, Kowalski, and Szykuła concerning relative extending words

                                         1    Introduction
                                         A complete deterministic finite automaton A with state set Q is synchronizing if some
                                         word maps all its states to one state. Put n = |Q|. If A is synchronizing, the least possible
                                         length of such a word is its reset threshold, denoted by rt(A). The Černý conjecture asserts
                                         that every synchronizing automaton with n states has reset threshold at most (n − 1)2 ; the
                                         classical examples of Černý show that this number would be best possible [5, Lemma 1]. As
                                         of 14 January 2026, Volkov’s living list recorded the general conjecture as neither proved
                                         nor disproved [14, Section 1.2]; see also [13, Section 3.1].
                                             Throughout, words act on states on the right: q · (uv) = (q · u) · v. For a subset X ⊆ Q
                                         and a word w, we write X · w for its image and Xw−1 = {q ∈ Q : q · w ∈ X} for its full
                                         preimage. These conventions are formalized in Section 2.
                                             An automaton with state set Q is one-cluster if it has a letter a whose functional
                                         digraph has connected underlying undirected graph; equivalently, that functional digraph
                                         has exactly one directed cycle. We call a a one-cluster letter, call its unique directed cycle

                                                                                        1
the a-cycle, and denote the vertex set of that cycle by C. The number of cycle states is
denoted by m = |C|. The level with respect to a is
                                       n                   o
                               ℓ = min j ≥ 0 : Q · aj = C .

Circular automata are the special case ℓ = 0; equivalently, C = Q and a acts on Q as a
cyclic permutation. Pin proved the conjecture for circular automata with a prime number
of states [10, Theorem 2], and Dubuc proved it for all circular automata [7, Proposition 4.6].
Synchronizing one-cluster automata admit quadratic reset bounds. In particular, Carpi
and D’Alessandro proved

                         rt(A) ≤ 2n2 − 4n + 1 − 2(n − 1) ln(n/2)

for the full class [4, Proposition 5 and Corollary 1]; see also the earlier bound in [2,
Proposition 2]. Steinberg proved the Černý bound when the unique cycle has prime length
[12, Theorem 8].
    Kisielewicz, Kowalski, and Szykuła conjectured the following stronger relative extending-
word property [9, Conjecture 1]. Every nonempty proper subset S ⊂ C should admit a
word w of length at most n for which the relative preimage S(waℓ )−1 ∩ C has cardinality
greater than |S|. They observed that this statement implies the Černý bound for the whole
synchronizing one-cluster class. The purpose of this paper is to prove their conjecture at
every positive level. It follows that

                         rt(A) ≤ (m − 1)(n − 1) + mℓ ≤ (n − 1)2 .

    The refined estimate is attained at positive level in every order n ≥ 4. In Section 10, we
construct strongly connected binary automata with m = 2, ℓ = n − 2, and reset threshold
3n − 5, exactly the right-hand side of the refined estimate. The estimate is not attained by
every positive-level family. For n > 2, the one-cluster family Dn′′ of Ananichev, Gusev, and
Volkov has m = n − 1, ℓ = 1, and rt(Dn′′ ) = (n − 1)(n − 2) [1, Figure 8 and Theorem 6].
Our estimate gives (n − 1)2 for this family, which is larger by n − 1.
    The statement is cycle-relative: it counts only preimages lying in C. It also bounds the
prefix w, rather than the whole word waℓ . These two features distinguish it from ordinary
extension and from Volkov’s 1-extensibility. The precise definitions and the relation among
the terminologies are given in Theorem 2.2.
    Steinberg’s prime-cycle proof uses centered characteristic vectors and an ascending-
chain argument within his averaging framework [12, equation (1), Lemma 2, Proposition 5,
Lemma 6, and Theorem 8]; see also the averaging lemma in [11, Lemma 2]. The present
proof retains that relative preimage framework but replaces the prime-cycle cyclotomic
irreducibility step by a Fitting decomposition and a roots-of-unity cancellation that also
applies to composite cycle lengths. The filtered spaces below refine a construction of Dubuc;
the precise specialization to the circular case is recorded when those spaces are defined.
    Unpublished workshop slides supplied by Costa outline joint work with Steinberg for
one-cluster automata having a single off-cycle state, that is, n = m + 1 and ℓ = 1 [6].
Like our proof, their argument filters transition rows by the number of letters different
from a. The outline identifies two difficulties at higher level: the nilpotent part of the
a-transition can have longer chains, and the required rows may no longer admit sufficiently
short word representatives. The Fitting decomposition, the two generation bounds, and
annular descent overcome these difficulties.
    The obstruction beyond level one is the subspace of complex row vectors annihilated by
a power of the transition operator of the one-cluster letter. After spectral projectors are

                                              2
inserted into a word product, a term may enter this nilpotent subspace and later return to
the invertible Fitting summand. A simplex of all exponent tuples up to one total degree
does not cancel all such terms. We instead omit a precisely determined lower simplex.
The remaining total degrees form an annular interval; when its lower endpoint is positive,
the interval has exactly m consecutive integers. This width permits the roots-of-unity
recurrence, and the shifted lower endpoint removes its uncancelled boundary coefficient.
The exact endpoints are defined in Section 5.
     The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the automata-
theoretic and matrix notation, states the main results, and introduces a five-state binary
automaton of level two that serves as a running example. Sections 3 to 5 develop the
linear-algebraic machinery: the Fitting decomposition, short actual-word generators for
filtered spaces, and the annular descent identity. Section 6 converts that identity into
a short relative extending word by positive-coefficient induction. Section 7 gives the
singleton start, and Section 8 completes the reset accounting and proves the main theorem.
Section 9 records structural features of the annular argument, while Section 10 constructs,
in every order at least four, a family attaining the refined estimate. The positive-level
proof is self-contained; only the circular boundary case invokes Dubuc’s theorem. The
Costa–Steinberg slides are cited as an unpublished proof outline and are not used as a
proof dependency.


2    Automata, matrices, and the main results
This section defines the word action, transition matrices, and basic one-cluster parameters,
and states the two results to be proved. The counting identity below is the bridge between
preimage growth and linear algebra.

is synchronizing if it has a reset word, and in that case its reset threshold rt(A) is the

    The support of A is the directed multigraph with vertex set Q and an edge q → q · g for
every q ∈ Q and g ∈ Σ; loops are allowed, and parallel edges carrying different input letters
are retained. The automaton is strongly connected if its support is strongly connected,
equivalently, if for every ordered pair p, q ∈ Q there is a word w with p · w = q.

Definition 2.1. For a ∈ Σ, its functional digraph, also called the a-skeleton [12, Section 1
and Figure 1], has vertex set Q and the single outgoing edge q −→ q · a at each state.

exactly one a-cluster, and then call A one-cluster with respect to a. The functional digraph
has a unique directed cycle, called the a-cycle; let C ⊆ Q be its vertex set. The elements
of C are cycle states, and those of Q \ C are off-cycle states.

    We now formalize the relative-extension terminology used in the statement. This is
the cycle-restricted version of the standard method of extension; the restriction to C is
essential at positive level.

Definition 2.2. Let ∅ ̸= S ⊊ C. A word u ∈ Σ∗ extends S relative to C if

                                         Su−1 ∩ C > |S| .

Equivalently, u is S-augmenting in the terminology of Béal and Perrin [2, Section 3]. If
u = waℓ , we refer descriptively to w as the prefix before the prescribed suffix aℓ ; the word
that extends S is the entire word u, not merely w.



                                                    4
     For comparison, if ∅ ̸= S ⊊ C, then a word u ∈ Σ∗ extends S in the ordinary sense
if Su−1 > |S|. Relative extension implies ordinary extension because Su−1 ∩ C ⊆ Su−1 ,
but the converse need not hold when C ̸= Q. When C = Q, the two notions coincide. At
positive level, Theorem 2.3 is not a 1-extensibility statement in Volkov’s sense: it imposes
the stronger cycle-relative inequality, while its bound applies to the prefix w and the
whole extending word may have length n + ℓ. Moreover, Volkov’s α-extensibility quantifies
over proper nonsingleton subsets of Q, whereas the relative statement here also includes
singleton subsets of C [13, Section 3.4].
     The restriction of the quantified subsets to C is substantive. If ordinary extension is
required for every proper nonsingleton subset of Q, Berlinkov’s one-cluster family Bn has
an obstruction: the shortest word extending {0, n − 1} is an−2 ban−2 , of length 2n − 3 [3,
Theorem 1]; see also [13, Section 3.4 and Figure 21]. For the distinguished one-cluster
letter in this family, C = {0, 1, . . . , n − 2}, so the obstructing set is not contained in C.
Thus the family rules out every uniform coefficient below 2 for ordinary extensibility, but
it does not conflict with the cycle-relative theorem proved here.
     The short relative-extension theorem, Theorem 2.3, is the local engine: it increases the
relative-preimage cardinality of a proper subset of the cycle using a word of the prescribed
form and gives a uniform bound on the prefix. Iterating that engine, after an economical
first extension from a singleton, gives the reset bound in Theorem 2.4.
Theorem 2.3 (Short relative extension). Let A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ be a synchronizing one-cluster
automaton with respect to a ∈ Σ. Let C be its a-cycle, put n = |Q| and m = |C|, and let ℓ
be its level with respect to a. Assume m ≥ 2 and ℓ ≥ 1. For every nonempty proper subset
S ⊂ C, there exists a word w ∈ Σ∗ such that

                            |w| ≤ n,       S(waℓ )−1 ∩ C > |S| .                            (4)

    Thus waℓ is a relative extending word in the sense of Theorem 2.2, and the bound in
(4) applies only to the prefix w. In particular, the whole relative extending word has length
at most n + ℓ.
Theorem 2.4 (Reset threshold). Let A = ⟨Q, Σ, δ⟩ be a synchronizing one-cluster automa-
ton with respect to a ∈ Σ. Let C be its a-cycle, put n = |Q| and m = |C|, and let ℓ be its
level with respect to a. Then

                         rt(A) ≤ (m − 1)(n − 1) + mℓ ≤ (n − 1)2 .                           (5)

Consequently, every synchronizing one-cluster automaton satisfies the Černý conjecture.
    The first inequality in (5) is attained at positive level in every order n ≥ 4; the examples
and their exact reset thresholds are given in Section 10.
    The proof of Theorem 2.4 separates two boundary cases from the positive-level argument.
If m = 1, the power aℓ is a reset word. If ℓ = 0, the required estimate is Dubuc’s circular-
automaton theorem [7, Proposition 4.6]. The formal proof in Section 8 records both
reductions.

3    Fitting and spectral decomposition
Before filtering words by their controls, we isolate the structure forced by the distinguished
letter a alone. The Fitting decomposition produces two complementary invariant subspaces:
the restriction of the distinguished transition is nilpotent on one and invertible of finite
order on the other. The associated polynomial projectors will be used both in filtered
generation and in the spectral-projector expansion.
    This decomposition is the positive-level analogue of the polynomial kernel–image
splitting used by Dubuc for circular automata [7, Lemma 3.1]. In the circular case only
factors of X m − 1 occur. At positive level the identity T ℓ (T m − I) = 0 introduces in
addition the zero-primary, nilpotent component isolated below.

a ∈ Σ, and retain the notation C, n, m, t, ℓ from Theorem 2.1. Let

For q ∈ Q, let eq ∈ V denote the standard basis row whose qth coordinate is 1 and whose
other coordinates are 0. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, every vector space, linear span,
and dimension in the paper is taken over C. Since every state is on C after ℓ applications
of a, and am is the identity on C, row by row we have

4    Filtered spaces and short actual-word generators
When ℓ = 0, so that C = Q, the rows and filtered spaces below coincide over C with
Dubuc’s construction for circular automata [7, Definition 4.2]. At positive level, the
auxiliary filtered spaces defined below separate the nilpotent part from the cycle-coordinate
part. This refinement has no nonzero nilpotent counterpart in the circular case.
    After fixing a letter f , we use a row that records the change of the cycle-indicator
row after applying f . We organize its right translates by the number of controls in the
translating word. The aim is to represent the resulting filtered spaces by actual words with
explicit length bounds.

9    Remarks on the annular mechanism
The proof is entirely finite-dimensional, but three features of the annular relation are worth
emphasizing.
    First, the two generation bounds in Theorem 4.2 play different roles. The total-
dimension bound contains kr = dim(Lr ∩ N ). In the second construction, every new control
uses the annihilating polynomial X ℓ ψr (X) and therefore adds at most ℓ beyond the growth
of the R-component. Taking the minimum of the two bounds produces κr = min(t, ℓr). For
a generator with r controls and exponent tuple hγ , the strict inequality τ (hγ ) ≤ Dr+ − 1
permits the case τ (hγ ) < Dr− to be shifted into the total-exponent interval by a positive
multiple of m.
    Second, the relevant nilpotent dimension bound is z + s, not merely the number z of
N -positions. For every j at which the term is not reduced to Mr−1 , Theorem 5.2 says that
the quotient classes

                        xj + Hj−1 , xj J + Hj−1 , . . . , xj J νj + Hj−1

are linearly independent. Summing the corresponding dimension increments gives (42),
which is the estimate used in (48).
    Third, when κr > r, the total-exponent interval contains exactly m integers:

                                       Dr+ − Dr− + 1 = m.

                                                26
The roots-of-unity generating function then compares coefficient indices d and d − m.
Replacing the lower endpoint Dr− by zero would remove the hd−m (Y ) term in (47) and
leave an uncancelled boundary coefficient. Thus both endpoints in (31) are required by
the interaction between the powers of J on N and the roots-of-unity spectrum on R.
     For ℓ = 1, we have J = 0, so there are no nontrivial nilpotent iterates. For ℓ = 2, the
first shifted total-exponent interval, at filtration index r = 1, is
                                          1 ≤ h0 + h1 ≤ m.
No step of the proof depends on the cycle length being prime.

u satisfies
                                        |u|b ≥ n − 1.                                   (68)
Proof. Let u = vg be a shortest reset word, where g ∈ {a, b} is its last letter. The word
v is not a reset word, so Qn · v contains at least two distinct states with the same image
under g. The only two distinct states merged by a are 0 and 2, and their common image is
1. When n is even, the only two distinct states merged by b are 0 and n − 1, again with
common image 1. When n is odd, b is a permutation and merges no two distinct states.
These three cases show that u resets to 1.
    For the letter count, track the images of the pair {0, 1} until its first merger, and
record whether the two numerical labels have equal or opposite parity. They initially have
opposite parity. The letter a reverses the parity of every state. The letter b also reverses
parity except at its final transition
                                                     (
                                                         1,   if n is even,
                                  (n − 1) · b =
                                                         0,   if n is odd,

                                                       28
whose two endpoints have the same parity. Thus the relation “equal parity” versus “opposite
parity” changes only when a b-step has exactly one tracked state at n − 1.
     At least one such exceptional b-step occurs no later than the first merger. Choose the
first one. If the merger uses a, then immediately beforehand the tracked states are 0 and 2
and have the same parity, so their parity relation changed earlier. If the merger uses b,
then n is even and the tracked states form {0, n − 1}; the merging step itself is the required
exceptional step.
     Immediately before the exceptional step, one tracked state is at n − 1. Before the first
merger, the two tracked states are distinct and their maximum numerical label is at least 1.
On such a pair, applying a does not increase the maximum: the image of 0 is 1, and every
positive label decreases by one. Applying b increases the maximum by at most one. Since
the initial maximum is 1, at least n − 2 occurrences of b are needed before one tracked state
can reach n − 1. The exceptional step is one further occurrence, which proves (68).

10.1    Even orders: two letter-counting invariants
Fix an even integer n ≥ 4. The common obstruction in Theorem 10.3 forces at least n − 1
occurrences of b. The remaining invariant uses full preimages to force at least 2n − 4
occurrences of a in a shortest reset word.
   For the second count, use the cyclic order

the empty set and Qn are also called cyclic intervals. For S ⊆ Qn , write si = 1 if i ∈ S

Lemma 10.4 (Even inverse-interval potential). Let n ≥ 4 be even, let a, b be the letters of
Fn , and let S ⊆ Qn be a cyclic interval in (69). Then Sa−1 and Sb−1 are cyclic intervals

Proof. Traverse the states in the order (69). Their images under a and b occur in the
respective cyclic lists

                            b:     1, 3, 5, . . . , n − 1, 2, 4, . . . , n − 2, 1.

An arithmetic progression in these lists is empty when its first displayed term exceeds its
last; for n = 4, the lists are 1, 1, 0, 2 and 1, 3, 2, 1. After one of the two cyclically adjacent
repetitions is deleted, the first list is a cyclic shift of (69) with n − 1 omitted, and the

positions either adds one position next to the block, when the repeated image belongs to
S, or adds no position. Thus the full preimage is a cyclic interval.

cases.

n − 2 and n − 1. This is impossible in (69): one of the two arcs between 0 and 1 contains
n − 2, and the other contains n − 1, while a cyclic interval containing both endpoints
contains at least one of these two arcs. Hence ι(Sa−1 ) = 0. Equation (73) now gives
Ψ(Sa−1 ) − Ψ(S) = 1.

Corollary 10.8 (Sharpness in every order). For every integer n ≥ 4, the automaton Fn
is strongly connected, binary, synchronizing, and one-cluster with m = 2 and ℓ = n − 2.
Moreover,
                         rt(Fn ) = 3n − 5 = (m − 1)(n − 1) + mℓ.

Proof. Strong connectivity and the one-cluster parameters were established after Theo-
rem 10.1. The upper bound is Theorem 10.2. The matching lower bound is Theorem 10.5
when n is even and Theorem 10.7 when n is odd.

Remark 10.9 (The orientation of the long cycle). For odd n, replace b in Theorem 10.1
by the inverse cycle q 7→ q − 1 (mod n). The relabelling q 7→ −q (mod n) sends the two
letter actions to those of the Wielandt-type automaton W(n, n, 2). Indeed, the inverse
cycle becomes r 7→ r + 1 (mod n), while the other letter becomes

             0 7−→ n − 1,      n − 1 7−→ 0,        r 7−→ r + 1   (1 ≤ r ≤ n − 2),

which are the defining transitions of that automaton [8, Section 2]. Since n is odd and
n ≥ 5, the integers 2 and n are coprime and n > 2. Therefore [8, Theorem 2.2] gives its
reset threshold as 2n − 3. Thus the relative orientation of the full n-cycle and the two-cycle
with its tail changes the reset threshold from 2n − 3 to 3n − 5.
